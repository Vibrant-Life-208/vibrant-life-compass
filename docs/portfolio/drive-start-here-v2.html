What this is

A three-year in-person studio formation for learners aged 16 to 18, run by one guide at Vibrant Life. It is not a high school. Each year asks one question. Origin asks Who am I? The Forge asks What is worth solving? The Helm asks What do I do when there is no easy answer? The years turn as a cycle rather than a ladder, so a learner who starts in the second year is not behind.

The week has a fixed shape. Tuesday and Thursday are guide days, four hours each, opening with a body-first settle and a real "how are you, really?" and carrying a shared meal. Monday and Wednesday the learners run their own mornings while the guide is on site with the younger children. Friday is fallow, never earned and never filled.

The six promises

These come before the inventory because they are what the programme is. Nothing in it is allowed to break them.

  1. No grades or scores anyone but you can see. Your progress is yours.
  2. No ranking and no leaderboards. You are never stacked against the person next to you.
  3. No one deposits the answers into you. The guide holds questions and walks with you.
  4. No imposed deadlines. You set your own pace, and "I didn't get to it" is honored, not punished.
  5. Your worth is not your output. A quest you abandoned and learned from counts as much as one you finished.
  6. No surveillance. The data of your life belongs to you, not to the school.

And one on top of the six: you may refuse the structure itself, and your no is real.

The wall: what is never shown

Ten things never appear in any outreach, post, page, poster, letter, talk or conversation. A learner's interior work as content. Transformation as proof. A learner's story, image, voice or likeness. Outcome promises or any number about a learner. A founder story whose engine is a specific child. Re-contacting a graduate for a testimonial. Urgency and scarcity. The pilot morning. Borrowed visual language of struggle. AI-generated imagery of children or the room.

The full wall is document 42 in folder 2. It governs every artifact that leaves the building, and the audit for it is described under How this page was checked.

Held by counsel, not self-certifiable

One engagement, three questions, and the long pole of the whole programme. Whether "on site and reachable" is sufficient for self-managed mornings with 16 to 18 year olds under Idaho and umbrella-school rules. What can be done for a learner who cannot hold three hours alone. What medical plans one adult with an infant and a CPR certification can honestly run. HELD ELSEWHERE: counsel. This is not inside the blocking table because it is not the guide's to close, and a gate list that silently absorbs other people's domains becomes a personal debt ledger.

Not blocking but dated: the oversubscription method needs a decision and an owner ahead of the January letters, because the door promises families will be told the method before applications open.

The collision, and its status. As drafted, the most relationally expensive months of the admissions sequence sit in the last trimester, and the written replies and countersignatures sit in the weeks after a birth. The founder leave-and-return plan ratified on 20 September 2026 won an unconditional rest clause, rest that does not depend on a checklist. The two documents have never been read against each other.

The recommendation on this page is that the admissions clock moves rather than the leave plan. That is a recommendation and not yet a ratified decision: no decision log carries it. It needs the founder's word, and the deciding date is before the January letters go out.

If something happens to the guide

There is no document. A family committing a child to three years with a solo guide will ask, and the honest answer today is that the Successor Envelope does not exist. It is the first item on the founder's owed list, to be written while rested and co-signed. It is also the answer to the hardest question at the door.

How this page was checked

Everything here is generated from the working repository rather than typed by hand. Two checks run inside the build, so neither depends on anyone remembering a command. Both last ran 13 October 2026 and both passed on all ten family-tier files.

  • Fleet-name check. Eighty-nine distinct internal names, across every family-tier file. The earlier version of this check covered eight names in one file, which was narrower than the claim it carried.
  • Wall check. Numbers about learners, urgency and scarcity, and outcome promises. One hit was found and inspected: the word unlock, in "the door closes and stays unlocked from the learner's side," which is the empty exit and the opposite of a promise. Benign.

What these checks do not establish. They are not a truth check. They cannot see a present-tense promise about a room that does not exist yet, an image of a learner, or whether the register suits the reader. The promise audit that found three unbuilt claims has been run against the guide tier and not against the family tier. Ambiguous one-word internal names are excluded from the pattern to avoid false alarms on ordinary prose, which is a known blind spot. And the checks run against the local files; the Drive copies are copies, and the local repository remains the record.

On the substrate. This is Google Drive. Google can read the content, holds the access list and holds the audit record, and revocation is a request rather than a guarantee. Nothing here is encrypted or zero-knowledge, and the tier separation is a sharing setting rather than a cryptographic wall. That is a reasonable choice for a working shelf, and it should be said out loud rather than assumed.
