# The Closing Feast - Boise's Tables

**A recurring Launch Pad ritual: close each session by sharing a neighbor's table - the people who cook near us, and their craft**
**Status:** v0.2 - revised after the binding Kira Nerys + Stirps cultural-respect pass (2026-08-21). **Cannot run until the gate below is cleared.**
**Companions:** studio-year-plan v0.3; the three-year spec's "Live & Love Life" thread; Session 1 day-by-day

## What changed in v0.2 (and why)

v0.1 was honoring in intention and extracting in construction - its travel grammar ("experience a food-world," "go outward," "the whole wide table") quietly built a visitor/destination hierarchy: majority-culture teens touring the margins. Kira and Stirps required:

- **Reframe from "travel outward to other food-worlds" to "widening the table of *us*"** - neighbors meeting neighbors. Boise's tables already *are* the learner's tables, and some of the tribe are from these very communities.
- **A trauma-safe story-invitation** - invite the cook and their craft, never the refugee's journey.
- **Purge the consumption vocabulary** - no "experience [a culture]," "food-world," "different foods," "beyond your own."
- **Two partner tiers** - Basque (near-peer) and refugee/immigrant-owned (high-care) are not one list.
- **Insider-learner sovereignty** and **S2 empty-state dignity.**
- **Depth over breadth** - return to fewer tables; return-patronage is the real reciprocity.
- **The Kira/Stirps sign-off is a binding pre-first-feast gate**, held *with the owner or their community broker present.*

## What it is

Each session closes with a shared meal at (or with) a neighbor's table - a family who cooks near us - honoring **the cook and their craft**, and building a relationship the tribe *returns to*, not a tasting they leave behind. Food and the person who makes it. A returning relationship with our neighbors, not a tour of them.

It carries The Table's belonging and the interviewing through-skill (family → stakeholders → mentors → the neighbors who feed our city). A cook who is a master of their craft, feeding a room of teenagers who came to honor that craft, is the *Midnight Gospel* "an ordinary life carries enormous meaning" lesson - pointed at **the daily dignity of feeding people**, never at anyone's survival story.

## The frame: widening the table of us

Boise's tables are not "beyond" the learner - they are the learner's own city. The arc across the year is not outward-from-self-to-others; it is **widening the circle of "us"** until it holds the whole neighborhood. Two consequences the design must honor:

- **Some of the tribe are from these communities.** A learner whose heritage is Afghan, Iraqi, Ethiopian, Nepali, Basque, Somali, Nigerian - one of the tables we visit - is not a tourist to their own culture. The design must never cast a community's own child as a visitor to their aunt's cooking.
- **This is neighbors meeting neighbors**, never the settled majority sampling the margins.

### Insider-learner sovereignty

A learner whose culture is featured **chooses their own standing** - host it with pride on their terms, quietly attend, or sit it out - and is **never turned into the exhibit** ("oh, tell us about *your* food"). Nothing about us without us, *including the fifteen-year-old.* The one whose culture is on the plate holds the say over whether and how they are named.

## The story is a gift, never the price (trauma-safe)

The single non-negotiable. Many of these owners arrived as refugees; asking someone to narrate their flight for a paying teen audience is trauma-as-exhibit, and it hides inside good intentions.

- **The invitation is to the cook and the craft** - a master of their cuisine, a business-builder, an artist. That is the honored role. The journey is **never** the ask.
- **No "tell us your journey" anywhere.** If an owner chooses to speak of how they came to Boise, that is their gift, freely given in the moment - received, never requested, never pressed for more.
- **Brief the teens to honor by *not* asking.** The lesson is how to be a respectful guest of someone whose life has held things you cannot imagine - taught by not demanding they relive them.
- **Point the meaning at the craft, not the flight** - the paella, the sambusa, the injera; the daily work of feeding people.
- **The food is the offering; the story is never the price of the partnership.** Sharing the plate alone, with no story, receives identical honor and pay.
- Use the **community brokers per-owner** to advise what is safe to invite and what is not.

## The learner's role

The teen **never "picks" or "presents" a culture** (no majority-culture teen interpreting a people to the room). The teen helps **host a guest** and prepares to **listen and ask well** - researches so they can ask good questions about the craft, not so they can explain a people. Host and listener, never interpreter.

## Reciprocity - beyond payment

Payment is the floor of reciprocity, not its body. Every feast must leave the owner genuinely better off:

- **Pay real custom** - a paid meal and/or a paid visiting-host fee. Never "come talk to our kids for free." *(The floor - necessary, not sufficient.)*
- **Return-patronage is the truest reciprocity** - the tribe becoming real repeat customers who bring their own families is worth more to a small restaurant than a one-time fee. Build it in as a named good.
- **Continuity** - the same tables across years; the studio a returning, known presence; the benefit compounding *toward the owners* over time. A one-time paid visit is a transaction; a multi-year relationship is reciprocity.
- **Reciprocal curiosity** - the owner is a person in a conversation, not a subject under examination; they get to ask the teens about their lives too.
- **Community-initiated standing, not just consultation** - ask the brokers and communities *"what would make this good for your families?"* and let the answer change the design. They have standing to shape it, decline it, or ask something in return.
- **Post-feast debrief with the owner** (via the broker) on whether it served *them.* Their answer - not the teens' feelings - is the measure.

## The inclusion floor

- Base food always provided; no learner ever pays-to-participate or is exposed for not bringing/affording.
- Allergen / dietary / religious safety planned in advance.
- **Presence, not consumption** - "taste what you like"; no one's plate is watched (ED-safe).
- **S2 (The Lineage) empty-state dignity** - "where you come from" must have a graceful path for the learner whose heritage is interrupted, mixed, adopted, or unknown: "a recipe you wish you had," "the food of the place you live now," "the meal your family eats every week." Interrupted lineage is still lineage; no emotional pay-to-participate.
- Cultural respect: honor the cuisine and its people; every family's food is real food; **name the specific people, never a regional lump** ("Eritrean," "Nepali," "Afghan (Herat)" - never "African," "Middle Eastern").

## Depth over breadth

Return to **two or three tables held across the year**, not seven one-off tastings. A second visit is worth more than a seventh first-visit - the second time, the teens are no longer an audience, they are becoming regulars, and regulars is a relationship. If the year's scope is genuinely many-tables-once, call it *respectful exposure*, not *relationship* - do not call a tour the deeper thing.

## Two partner tiers (they are not one list)

**Tier A - The Basque community (near-peer commercial partner).** Established, multi-generational, culturally secure, with its own institutions. Power gap is nearly level; low trauma proximity. Engage directly; the cooking-class model is *right* here (it's a service they already sell).
- **The Basque Market** - family-run since 2006 (Tony & Tara Eiguren); already runs cooking classes - a natural teaching partner.
- **Leku Ona** - family-owned, founded by Jose Maria Artiach (born in the Basque Country).
- The Basque Block, Basque Museum & Cultural Center.

**Tier B - Refugee- and immigrant-owned tables (high-care protocol).** Recent arrival, precarious footing, live trauma proximity. **Broker through resettlement agencies; food-and-craft framing only; journey never requested; continuity required.** Leads to verify (status/ownership change - confirm each is open, owner-run, and willing before counting on it):
- **Sunshine Spice Bakery & Café** - Afghan (the Shams sisters).
- **Kibrom's Ethiopian & Eritrean** - family-owned since 2016 (Kibrom Milash & Tirhas Hailu).
- **Food Land Market / Ishtar** - Iraqi (Hana Mutlak). **Tarbush Kitchen** - Iraqi (Bunyan family). **Taste of Persia** - Afghan (Karim family).
- **Mount Everest Momo Cafe** - Nepali. **Amina's** - Somali sambusas. **Nahm** - Thai & Burmese. **Taste of Nigeria** - Nigerian (Zainab Abimbola).

**The ready scaffold:** Visit Boise's **International Cuisine Trail** - 40+ locally-owned restaurants from cultures worldwide, already curated.

**Community brokers (partner through these first, for warm and culturally-brokered outreach):** Idaho Office for Refugees; Agency for New Americans; City of Good.
