# The Launch Pad - The Closing Practice (the learner-held re-crossing)

**A cross-year companion practice for the 16-18 studio - the one addition Ring 1 asked for**
**Status:** v0.1 working draft - builds the single element the 2026-08-28 Ring-1 review (PDC + SSC + COC) said to *add* (everything else was hold-or-cut)
**Runs:** all three years (Origin / The Forge / The Helm), at three scales; introduced young in Origin, spiraled, and carried out the door at the Launch
**Companions:** the three-year spec ("Ring-1 revisions"); the Reasoning Standard (Helm S2 - this practice is its emotional twin); the somatic floor; the coach-not-therapist referral pathway; the Captain's Log

> **Why this exists.** Ring 1's most-converged finding, reached independently by seven structural angles: *the design opens the interior faster than it closes it.* The Launch Pad has world-class opening practices - 100 dreams, lineage, the worst-day autobiography, imposter work, mortality, the hard call - and, until now, only *local* closing (de-roling closes a scene; the glad-close closes a day). Nothing taught the learner to close the *arc* of what got opened, and three years of opened interior poured into a Log no one ever helps them read. This practice is the missing inverse operator. **The rule it serves: open no faster than you teach a person to close - and teach them to close it *themselves*, so the capacity leaves with them.**

---

## What it is (in one breath)

A small, **learner-held, never-read** practice for closing an opening - three prompts, shaped as a *re-crossing* (you went somewhere; you come back changed and whole). It is the emotional twin of the Reasoning Standard: the Reasoning Standard is how a learner closes a *thought* (reason it, own the cost, honor the dissent); the Closing Practice is how a learner closes a *feeling that got opened.* It is a **portable skill**, not a worksheet the studio keeps - the entire point is that the eighteen-year-old walks out able to run it on themselves, for the rest of their life, with no guide in the room.

---

## The practice - three prompts (a re-crossing)

After an opening - a dream named, a lineage touched, a hard call reasoned, a role stepped out of, a grief surfaced - the learner takes a quiet moment (alone, in the Log or in their head) and crosses back through three questions:

1. **What did I meet?** *(Name the thing that opened - the dream, the fear, the memory, the weight. Naming it is the first half of holding it.)*
2. **What do I carry back?** *(The elixir. It can be small; it can be "only the knowledge that I could stand in that and not be swept away." An honest "I carry back more questions than I brought" is a real answer.)*
3. **Who am I on this side that I wasn't on the other?** *(The change of standing, self-witnessed. Sometimes the honest answer is "the same, and steadier for having looked." That counts.)*

That is it. Three lines. van Gennep's rite of passage - separation, threshold, return - compressed into something a fifteen-year-old can do in ninety seconds and a launched adult can do at a kitchen table at 2 a.m. for the rest of their life.

---

## The three scales (close at the scale you opened - Data's rule)

An opening must be closed at its own scale. The practice runs at three:

- **Session/day scale** - the everyday close. After a Launch day's quest, or a self-guided day that opened something, the three prompts land the day. This is where the reflex is *built*, by low-stakes reps.
- **Season scale** - the harvest's private return. Every public harvest (the Unveiling, the Call to Action, the Council) is an *opening onto the social plane*; it needs a private re-crossing afterward, so the exhibited self comes back into the sovereign interior. And between seasons: a fallow integration window before the next season opens (never walk a new opening on a body still activated from the last harvest).
- **Arc scale** - the Launch rite itself *is* the arc-scale closing practice: Separation -> Liminality -> Reincorporation is the three prompts made into a ceremony for the whole three years. The learner who has run the practice for three years arrives at the Launch already fluent in the move the rite performs.

**The non-graduating learner needs the arc-scale close too** - between seasons, not only at the Launch. Build the between-season integration fallow as the arc-scale practice for the learner who is staying.

---

## How it is held (the invariants - these are floor, not furniture)

- **Learner-held and never read.** Like the Captain's Log, the closing practice is the learner's own. A guide never reads it for assessment. It is *how the learner reads themselves* - which is the resolution to the unread-Log question: the Log stays sovereign, and the learner is taught the practice that integrates what they put in it.
- **Rides the fallow; never a deliverable.** It lives in the fallow and the quiet, never graded, never harvested, never a thing to perform. A closing practice turned into a submitted worksheet has become another opening-for-display.
- **Portable is the point.** Teach it explicitly as a skill they keep. Name it: "this is yours to take - when you're forty and something cracks you open, these three questions are how you come home." The measure of success is not a good entry; it is a graduate who still uses it.
- **Introduced at a young register, spiraled.** In Origin it is light and concrete ("what did you meet in your 100 dreams today? what do you carry?"). By the Forge it deepens (closing the shadow-of-the-problem, the service exposure). By the Helm it is theirs, and it closes assisted-death allegory and *Our Town* and the Council.

---

## What it is NOT (the boundaries)

- **It is not de-roling.** De-roling closes an *embodiment* - stepping out of a role that was never you (Helm S4 theatre; say your name, name three true things about your real life, shed the prop). The closing practice closes an opening of *the self that truly was you*. **Do not use the de-roling mechanic on real grief** (Guinan's caution) - de-roling teaches "that wasn't me"; grief needs "that was me, and I carry it and I am still here." Different tools; keep the line bright.
- **It is not the Reasoning Standard.** The Reasoning Standard closes a *dilemma* (cognitive). This closes a *feeling* (affective). A learner may need both after a heavy session - reason the hard call, *and* re-cross what it stirred.
- **It is not therapy, and it does not replace the trap door.** Sometimes a close surfaces something bigger than a close can hold - a grief that won't settle, a memory that opens a wound, a signal in the coach-not-therapist §3 list. When "what did I meet?" meets something that needs real help, the practice hands off to the **referral pathway**, warmly and without shame. The closing practice is the everyday return; the pathway is for the openings too big to close alone.

---

## Guide notes

- **Teach it, then get out of the way.** The guide models it out loud a few times (genuinely - "what I met today, what I carry, who I am on this side"), then it becomes the learner's private reflex. The guide's job is to *make the space and the silence*, not to hear the answers.
- **Name the close, don't demand the content.** "Take your closing minute" is the whole prompt. What they met and carry is theirs; the invitation to close is the only thing offered, and even that can be declined (decline-with-reasoning applies - the guide has a voice, never a veto).
- **The somatic floor carries it.** The three prompts ride on the body's return (settle -> discharge -> return). A learner who is flooded cannot re-cross cognitively; regulate the body first (K'Ehleyr's floor), then the three questions land.
- **Watch the tender openings.** After the heaviest material, the close is where a struggling learner's state can surface. Hold it as the SSC's non-invasive practice: the learner-commanded flag ("I want to talk about this") routes to care, never correction; you see the flag, never the content, unless invited.
