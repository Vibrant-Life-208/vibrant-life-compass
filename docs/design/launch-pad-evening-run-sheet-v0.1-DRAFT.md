**DRAFT. The design is settled; the clock below is not.** The content comes straight from the promotion document §3, which says what the evening is. The timings, the order and the words to say are proposed here for the first time and have not been read by anyone. Accord owes the exhale read on this before it is used.

**What it is:** one evening, "come see the room." Repeated once if asked. **No learners present, on display, or helping.** Ninety minutes.

**The point of it:** families *do* the things rather than watch a description of them. Everything below is something they do with their own hands and mouths.

---

### In the room before anyone arrives

- A low-sensory seat, already there. **Not offered on request.** Chekov's condition: built into the room rather than produced when someone asks for it.
- A written version of everything that will be said aloud, on the Table, enough copies for everyone.
- The Table laid as it is on a guide day. Something simple to eat.
- The three books out, open, touchable. Nobody introduces them.
- Nothing on the walls about a learner. Nothing about outcomes.

---

### The ninety minutes

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **0:00 - 0:15** | **Arrive, and nothing happens.** | Door open, tea, the books on the Table. No greeting line, no name badges, no sign-in sheet. Let people stand about. This is the walk-in, and it is the first honest thing about the week. |
| **0:15 - 0:20** | **The settle, done by them.** | "This opens every guide morning. Thirty seconds. Feet on the floor, look around the room, one breath. You may sit it out." Then do it with them, and do not comment on it afterwards. |
| **0:20 - 0:40** | **One choice game.** | One round of the real thing from Session 3. Parents play it, not watch it. The point they will feel without being told: nobody is scored and nobody is made to go. |
| **0:40 - 1:05** | **The Table.** | Sit down and eat. The guide sits and eats too. No presentation over food. If questions come, answer them at the table rather than saving them for a slot. |
| **1:05 - 1:20** | **The refusals, read aloud.** | Read the six and the seventh, in the words they are written in, from the page. Do not paraphrase them warmer. Then the one sentence that makes them real: *"these are promises to your learner, and they can hold us to them."* |
| **1:20 - 1:30** | **The limits, and how you leave.** | One guide, an infant on guide days, no aide. Self-run Monday and Wednesday mornings. A record no institution yet recognises. The price. How you leave, and that leaving costs nothing. Then stop talking. |

---

### What this evening is not allowed to become

- **No learner is present, named, quoted or shown.** Not as a greeter, not in a photo, not in a story. Wall item 3.
- **No outcome is promised.** If someone asks what their kid will get, the honest answer is the conditions we promise and the outcomes we do not.
- **No urgency, no scarcity.** "The room has seven chairs" is a fact when asked and a lever when it leads. It never leads. No deadline is announced at this evening.
- **Nobody is asked to decide anything, sign anything, or leave contact details.** If a family wants a next step they will ask for one.

---

### Still to settle before this is usable

1. **The timings above are proposed, not tested.** Ninety minutes may be twenty too long for a Tuesday evening with teenagers at home.
2. **Who is the second adult in the room**, and what they do while the guide talks.
3. **What happens if a learner comes anyway**, brought by a parent who did not read the line. The answer cannot be turning a sixteen-year-old away at a door, and it is not written yet.
4. **Childcare**, or its absence, stated in the invitation rather than discovered on the night.
