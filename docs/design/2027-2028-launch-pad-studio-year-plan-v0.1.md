## Part 1 - The shape of the year

### The cycle, and where Year 1 sits

Launch Pad is a three-year expedition run as a cycle: **Origin** (Who am I?) - **The Forge** (What is worth solving?) - **The Helm** (What do I do when there is no easy answer?). A learner walks all three before they launch. This year runs **Origin**.

### The weekly rhythm

| Mon | Tue | Wed | Thu | Fri |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Self-guided** | **Guide-led** | **Self-guided** | **Guide-led** | **Fallow / hearth** |
| Learner is their own manager | Socratic depth, conversation, group challenge | Learner is their own manager | Socratic depth, conversation, group challenge | Protected. Never filled. |

*Note: this places the guide-led days on Tuesday and Thursday (the three-year spec had them on Mon/Wed). The A-B-A-B rhythm holds: self-guided Monday flows into Tuesday's Launch, whose Bridge feeds Wednesday, whose work meets Thursday's Launch, whose Bridge rests over Friday and lands the next Monday. Worth noting the day-swap in the guide spec so the two docs do not drift.*

| | **Self-guided - Mon & Wed** | **Guide-led - Tue & Thu** |
|---|---|---|

## Part 2 - Equipping the self-manager

The hard truth up front: **you cannot hand a 16-year-old two free days and expect self-management to appear.** Freedom without scaffolding produces paralysis or drift, and then everyone concludes "these kids can't handle freedom." They can. They have to be equipped for it, on purpose, with the training wheels coming off in stages.

### The gradual-release arc

| Phase | When | Self-guided days feel like... | Guide's posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 - Structured | S1-S4 (Aug-early Nov) | A clear template, a required weekly plan, visible checklists. Wheels on. | Reviews each learner's plan at the start of every guide day. Teaches the habit. |
| 2 - Guided freedom | S5-S6 (Nov-Feb) | Learner drafts their own week; accountability partners active. Wheels loosening. | Spot-checks. Coaches pacing. Steps back. |
| 3 - Real autonomy | S7 + Open Waters (Feb-May) | Learner runs their own weeks, fully. | Only if asked. The absence is now the lesson. |

**S4 "Running My Own Ship" is where self-management is formally taught** (time, routine, communication) - but the scaffold must exist from Week 1, because they are managing themselves before S4 arrives. Light tools day one; deep mastery at S4; full ownership by S7.

### The weekly planning loop (the load-bearing habit)

The guide-led days feed the self-guided days through the **Bridge** (last 20 min of every Tue/Thu Launch):

- **Thursday's Bridge** -> each learner writes their plan for **Monday** (Friday stays fallow - plan not to work it)
- **Tuesday's Bridge** -> each learner writes their plan for **Wednesday**

No learner ever walks into a self-guided day with a blank slate. They walk in with a plan they built two days earlier, while the Guide was still in the room to help shape it. That is the difference between "go do independent work" (fails) and "execute the plan you built with support" (works).

### The learner's toolkit

1. **The Weekly Plan** - their operational planner, separate from the private Captain's Log (template in Appendix A). Built at the Bridge, executed solo.
2. **The Credit Map** - their own tracker for HS core (template in Appendix B, see Part 4). They own it; the Guide witnesses it.
3. **Deep-work cycles** - 90-minute focused blocks with real breaks. Taught explicitly; most teens have never been shown how to sustain a block without a screen interrupting.
4. **The Stuck Protocol** - what to do when blocked and the Guide is off the floor.
5. **An accountability partner** - a peer they check in with at the start and end of each self-guided day. A witness, not surveillance.

### The Stuck Protocol (post it on the wall)

> When you are stuck: (1) Try it a different way for 10 minutes. (2) Check your notes and resources. (3) Ask your accountability partner. (4) Park it - move to a different task and come back. (5) Still stuck? Write it down for the next guide day. **Being stuck is not a failure. Staying stuck silently is the only mistake.**

### Accountability without surveillance

The Guide reviews **the plan and the learner's own reflection**, never surveils raw output (refusal #6 - the data belongs to the learner). Accountability is a conversation - *did you do what you said, and if not, what happened?* - not a gradebook. A missed self-guided day is recoverable and the plan flexes. A foggy week is not a character failure. The plan serves the learner; it is never a stick.

### The guide-led day (Tue & Thu), 9:00-1:30

| Time | Block |
|---|---|
| 9:00-10:00 | Walk-in & Project Hour - soft, self-directed start; grace for latecomers. Guide present, not directing. (Early in the year, Guide runs quick 1:1 credit-audit / pacing check-ins here while others read or work.) |
| 10:00-10:25 | Opening Circle - the body-first settle + "how are you, really?" |
| 10:25-10:40 | The Quest Frame - open the session's quest as a question, not a lecture. |
| 10:40-11:45 | The Work of the Day - the core activity (changes each session). |
| 11:45-12:15 | The Table - a shared meal. |
| 12:15-1:10 | Apply & Collaborate - carry the quest into hands-on and group work. |
| 1:10-1:30 | The Bridge + Glad Close - build the next self-guided day's plan; end with something light. |

**Going deep - the three modes you move between inside the Work of the Day:**

- **Socratic questioning.** You almost never answer; you ask the next question. "What makes you say that?" "What would have to be true for the opposite to be right?" "Who does this help, and who does it cost?" Hold the silence after a question - the first to talk should rarely be you. (Refusal #3: they co-create the meaning; the Guide does not deposit it.)
- **Real conversation.** Not discussion-that-performs. The Opening Circle trains the room to be real before the content, so honesty is already the norm when you reach the hard question.
- **Group challenges.** The kinetic, collaborative version - sorting games, the constellation, the potluck, later the mystery and Forum Theatre. Every guide day should have at least one moment where they are up, moving, or building together.

**Discussion culture (set Week 1, holds all year):** pass is always allowed; disagree with the idea, never the person; "I changed my mind" is a win; the quietest voice gets drawn out on purpose.

## Part 4 - High school core credits: the Year 1 rule

**If finishing core high-school credits is high on a learner's journey, they must be completed in Year 1.**

### Why Year 1

Front-loading the credits **frees Years 2 and 3.** The Forge (choose a real problem, run a service project) and the Helm (moral reasoning, mentorship, leadership) are the deep formative work; they should not compete for oxygen with a learner still chasing transcript credits. And Origin's own theme - "Running My Own Ship," routine, finishing HS requirements - already pulls in the same direction. Origin and credit completion agree. Forge and Helm should not have to.

### How it fits sovereignty (the honest part)

This is the one place a real deadline lives, and it brushes against refusal #4 (no imposed external deadlines). The reconciliation is honest, not papered over:

- **It is the learner's chosen goal, not an imposition.** Not every learner will put core credits high on their journey; some are on genuinely different paths, and that is real. The rule applies *if they have named it as theirs.*
- For the learner who has: the studio's job is to **help them make it** - pacing, support, problem-solving - not to hang it over them. The deadline is real because their goal is real, and we honor the goal by helping them hit it.

Say it plainly, Day 1 and again at the credit audit: *"If a diploma matters to your launch, this is the year we finish it - so the next two years are yours for the work only you can do."*

### The mechanics

1. **Credit audit - first two weeks.** Because it must be done Year 1, the plan cannot wait. In S1-S2, each learner (with the Guide) maps required credits, current standing, and what is left. This becomes their **Credit Map** (Appendix B).
2. **Pacing plan.** Spread remaining credits across ~30 weeks of Mon/Wed deep-work Block 1 + guide-day project time. Front-load - heavier in the fall while the season theme supports it, lighter by spring as the Unveiling ramps.
3. **Monthly checkpoint.** Review the Credit Map against the plan; the Guide witnesses and helps re-pace, never grades. The **S4 midpoint reflection (Nov 1 week)** is the natural formal check: *on track to finish core this year, and if not, what changes now?*
4. **Reality check, named Week 1.** A learner starting far behind may need summer work or a heavier front-load - an honest conversation to have early, not a surprise in April. Roughly 2 to 2.5 days a week of academic runway across 30 weeks is enough for a focused year of core *if* they start from a workable baseline and front-load.

## Part 5 - Day-by-day openers

### DAY 1 - Monday, August 16, 2027, 12:00-2:00 pm

*"Welcome to the Launch Pad" - orientation, expectations, Origin intro, tribe building.*
*Late start (12-2) because the Guide is with the Tots until 11:30. Day 1 is the one Monday the Guide is present; every other Monday is learner-led.*

**The four jobs of today, in the order that works for teens:** (1) belonging first, (2) the map of the year, (3) expectations both directions, (4) a taste of Origin. Do not hand down rules on Day 1; lead with welcome, show the shape, then build the expectations *with* them.

**Prep:** The Table set with real food; a blank Captain's Log at each seat; a question-card at each seat; wall space for the tribe's agreements; the year-map visual (one page, big); open floor for the constellation activity.

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 12:00-12:15 | Walk-in & The Table | Arrive at their own pace. Food out. A question-card at each seat to answer low-key while people trickle in. No pressure. |
| 12:15-12:35 | Opening Circle | The first "how are you, really?" + a name round. You go first, and honest. Name that "pass is always allowed here." |
| 12:35-1:00 | The Map of the Year | The big story: three seasons, the weekly rhythm, the Captain's Log, the harvests and feasts. A story, not a syllabus. |
| 1:00-1:20 | The Two-Way Deal | What this place will NOT do to you (the six refusals) -> then co-write the tribe's agreements for each other. |
| 1:20-1:40 | What Origin Is | "Who am I?" the season's question. Name the Log. Plant the first dream-spark. Preview Tuesday. |
| 1:40-1:55 | Tribe Building - "The Constellation" | Call out spectrums; learners physically place themselves. Reveals connection with no forced disclosure. |
| 1:55-2:00 | The Bridge + Glad Close | "Between now and Tuesday, notice what you daydream about - that's data. Bring it." End light. |

**Question-card options:** "What's one thing you're actually good at that no class ever graded?" / "If this year went better than you dared hope, what would be different by summer?"

**The Two-Way Deal, said as a promise first:** *"Here is what this place will not do to you: no grades or scores anyone but you can see; no ranking you against each other; we will not hand you answers and call it learning; no deadlines dropped from above - you set your pace, and 'I didn't get to it' is a real answer here; your worth is never your productivity; your Log is yours, and no one reads it to judge you, ever."* Then flip it: *"So what do we owe each other?"* Capture their words on the wall. It is their list.

**The Constellation:** light first ("morning person / night person - stand where you fall"), then commonality ("move here if you've made something you were proud of" / "if music runs your moods"), then one with gentle depth ("stand between 'I know exactly who I am' and 'I have no idea' - wherever's honest today"). Then name it: *"You keep landing near people you didn't expect. That's your tribe."* Previews S1's Map of Me and seeds the constellation motif; nobody had to confess anything.

**Name the Log:** they title their own Captain's Log today, and make a two-minute first entry. Small. Theirs.

### DAY 2 - Tuesday, August 17, 2027, 9:00 am-1:30 pm

*S1 "The Map of Me" opens. The first true guide-led Launch. Work of the Day: 100 Dreams, unfiltered.*
*This is the first full-length day and the first real taste of the studio rhythm. It sets the tone for every guide day that follows.*

**The one job of today:** get 100 dreams out of them with the ceiling removed - and make the room feel safe enough to dream honestly in front of each other.

| Time | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 9:00-10:00 | Walk-in & Project Hour | First taste of self-directed time. Since no Weekly Plans exist yet (Day 1 was orientation), point them at reading or getting set up. **The Guide uses this hour for quick 1:1 credit-audit kickoffs** - "is finishing core credits high on your journey this year? Let's start your Credit Map" - rotating through 2-3 learners while the rest read or settle. |
| 10:00-10:25 | Opening Circle | The settle + "how are you, really?" Second time doing it; the ritual starts to feel like theirs. Anyone can pass. |
| 10:25-10:40 | The Quest Frame | Open 100 Dreams as a question, not an assignment: *"What would you want your one life to hold if nothing were stopping you?"* Name the rule: no filtering, no being realistic, no ceiling. |
| 10:40-11:45 | The Work of the Day - 100 Dreams | Write 100 dreams. Fast, unjudged, no editing. (Facilitation below.) |
| 11:45-12:15 | The Table | Shared meal. Belonging, not a working lunch. |
| 12:15-1:10 | Apply & Collaborate | Voluntary share-out + "dream neighbors": find someone whose list rhymes with yours. Build a shared wall of dreams. Keep adding toward 100. |
| 1:10-1:30 | The Bridge + Glad Close | Build the Wednesday self-guided plan: carry the list toward 100, start the Credit Map, reading. End with a laugh or one glad word each. |

**Facilitating 100 Dreams (the heart of the day):**

- **Why 100, and why unfiltered.** The number is the method. The first 20-30 dreams are the obvious, safe, borrowed ones. Around 30-40 the easy list runs dry - and that is exactly where the true, strange, real dreams start to surface. Tell them this so the wall (which will come) is expected, not discouraging: *"When you feel stuck around 40, keep going - the best ones are on the other side of that wall."*
- **Remove judgment, keep momentum.** During the block the Guide does not evaluate a single dream. You keep them generating: "Write it, don't weigh it." No dream is too big, too small, too silly, too impractical.
- **Prompts to break blocks** (call them out when the room slows): places to go, things to learn, things to make, people to meet, who you want to become, what you want to give, adventures to have, what you want to be brave enough to try, an ordinary day you'd love to live. The 12 life categories are your prompt bank - career, friendship, health, adventure, family, faith, learning, contribution, and the rest.
- **The learner who says "I don't have dreams."** Normalize it - some kids have had dreams shut down and the muscle is stiff. Never push. Offer a doorway: *"What did you want when you were eight? Start there."* Or let them borrow: *"Steal any dream you hear today that lights you up. Borrowed dreams count - they're how you find your own."*
- **Hold the vulnerability.** Dreaming out loud is exposing at 16. No one has to share, ever (pass stands). Some dreams will carry real longing or old hurt; the Guide's job is a steady, unjudging presence, not a probe. This is a generative day, not a disclosure day.
- **Anchor (guide-facing, unstated to learners):** Covey - all things are created twice, first mentally then physically. This dream-set is the mental creation the whole three years will physically build toward. The far-horizon dreams are the "end" to begin with.

**The Bridge into Wednesday (self-guided):** each learner leaves with a written plan - Block 1: continue the 100 Dreams draft and open your Credit Map; Block 2: reading / personal setup; close with a Captain's Log entry. This is the first Weekly Plan they build, so the Guide walks the room and helps shape each one before they leave - modeling the habit that carries all year.
