# The Launch Pad - Life Admin Strand (do-it-for-real, just-in-time)

**A cross-year companion strand for the 16-18 studio - the practical "adulting" layer**
**Status:** v0.5 working draft - from the 2026-08 Europa + Cura brainstorm; pressure-tested live (v0.2-v0.4 built and hardened the strand; **v0.5 applies the ratified Ring-1 leadership fixes (2026-08-28): the Studio Commons cut to its smallest-true-thing and moved to Year 2; "the documents that are you" killed and reframed; the two-skies unification and the learner-held closing practice noted**)
**Runs:** all three years (Origin / The Forge / The Helm), on the self-guided project days - a light exposure-touch on every item distributed across the three years, with deeper do-it-for-real dives pulled just-in-time and front-loaded into the launch year
**Companions:** the three studio-year plans; Helm S5 "The Arena of Voices" (real adults); the Growth Record; the existing dark-shipped Compass Life Skills / Financial Literacy courses

> This is not a course. It is the practical life-admin layer the Socratic studio work otherwise never touches - taxes, insurance, a lease, navigating officialdom - delivered the way Compass says learning actually sticks: *do it for real, once, with a guide beside you, at the moment it is actually due.* **It is not a separate world from the three seasons: it is the Reasoning Standard applied to the concrete world - the fourth room of the same house (The Doctor). A lease is a Kobayashi Maru at small scale; a phishing teardown is the Detective's 5 Whys.**

## The design principle

Most life-skills curricula teach that taxes exist. The real gap is not knowledge - it is the terror of the first form, done alone, with real money on the line. That is the Compass thesis exactly: **the gap is discipline and doing, not knowledge.** Nobody stumbles because they did not know insurance existed; they stumble because the first time they had to pick a plan there was no one beside them and the stakes were real.

So the strand's whole shape is:
- **Do it for real, not a class about it.** "Today we file your taxes together" beats a Financial Literacy unit every time - a real artifact, a real task, a guide beside you.
- **Just-in-time, not just-in-case.** Each skill is tied to the moment it is actually needed. A comprehensive "here is all of adulthood" course glazes eyes; the same content at the moment of need sticks for life.
- **Learner-pulled, guide-offered.** The learner's real life surfaces the need (at the ~12:00 check-in); the guide offers the real task. Not pushed on a schedule.
- **Exposure for all, depth by choice.** Everyone gets a light touch on every item (so no one launches never having heard of it); the deeper do-it-for-real dive is the learner's to take - or to decline *with a reasoned why* (below). No silent dodging.

## The engagement model - no silent dodge (decline-with-reasoning)

The resolution to the completeness-vs-sovereignty tension, and the heart of v0.2. Two layers, one rule:

- **Exposure is non-negotiable; depth is sovereign.** Every learner *learns what each item is* (the distributed exposure-days below). Whether they then do the deeper, real dive is theirs to choose.
- **The rule: you may decline the dive, but you name why.** Not a graded checkbox and not a free pass - a *reasoned refusal.* "I've looked at this, and I'm not diving deeper because ___." This is a Kohlberg-Level-6 move; it plugs straight into the S2 Reasoning Standard (name your principle, own the cost).
- **It marries the "right kind of scared."** The reasoned "why" surfaces the difference between a genuine path-choice ("I will never drive, so car insurance is not mine to chase" - honored) and avoidance-fear ("this scares me"). If it is the right kind of scared - bigger than you, and you keep circling it - the honest naming *is* the nudge to do it anyway. If it is a real no, the reasoning honors it. Fear-as-compass, applied to the learner's own life-admin.
- **The guide has a voice, never a veto (Europa).** The guide cannot reject or overrule a learner's reasoning - they may only Socratically question it ("what happens the first time you *do* need this and no one is beside you?"). The learner's reasoned no stands after the questioning. A guide who can reject a reason has turned decline-with-reasoning back into compliance; the Socratic question is a mirror, not a gate.

This keeps sovereignty whole (no one is forced to *do*, and no reason is adjudicated) while ensuring no one dodges out of unexamined avoidance - and it turns the readiness map from a compliance tracker into a record of *reasoned choices*.

## The three triggers (how "now" gets decided)

Just-in-time only works if something reliably fires the "now" signal. Three ways a life-skill becomes due:

1. **Life-event triggered** (per-learner, asynchronous). The learner hits a real threshold and the check-in catches it: *"I got a job offer" -> this week we do your real W-4 and read your first pay stub.* *"We're getting an apartment" -> we read your actual lease line by line.* Got a car, turned 18, applying to college, a health scare - each pulls its task. This is the spine.
2. **Calendar-anchored** (cohort-wide, seasonal). Some things have a real clock: tax season (Jan-Apr), FAFSA (opens October), voter registration before an election, open enrollment. These become shared project-day clusters at the real moment - everyone files a return in March, once, together.
3. **Launch-anchored** (final year). The cluster *everyone* needs before they walk out, front-loaded into The Helm: the documents audit, the health-coverage transition, the full independent-living stack, the Launch-Floor sweep (below).

## The "do it for real" ladder (because most learners are minors)

A 16-year-old cannot sign a lease. So "for real" scales three ways, and the guide picks the highest rung that is legal-and-real for that learner:
- **Real now, any age:** open a real bank account, read a real pay stub, track a real month's budget, get a state ID, register to vote at 17.5, spot a real phishing email, cancel a real subscription, read a real Terms of Service.
- **Real artifact, simulated stakes:** file a *realistic* return with the actual IRS forms and sample (or their own) numbers; walk the real ACA marketplace and price real plans; read a real lease.
- **Real at the threshold:** their *actual* first-job W-4, their *actual* FAFSA, their *actual* apartment lease - done with the guide the week it lands.

**Privacy floor:** "do it for real" defaults to the learner's *own* real life plus realistic simulations. Family data (the family's lease, the family's taxes) is touched only with the family's consent - the learner's own bank account is theirs; the family's finances are not.

## The Launch Floor - eight things, done once, before you go

The Floor is the small, non-negotiable set nobody should leave without having done *once*. Each earns its place only by clearing four bars: **high cost to stumble on, near-universal regardless of path, genuinely paralyzing the first time alone, and easy to dodge until it bites you.** Everything else stays genuinely just-in-time. The Floor is **offered as care - part of being handed the keys - never graded, never a deadline, never ranked.** "Before you go, we make sure you have touched these once, together."

1. **Your paycheck & taxes.** Read a real pay stub (gross vs net, FICA, deductions); file one real or realistic return. *(A refund means you overpaid.)* Do-it: file via Free File.
2. **Banking & credit - money you hold and owe.** Hold a real account; pull and read your own free credit report; understand how a score is built from zero and how credit-card interest compounds against you. Do-it: open/hold an account; read your real credit report.
3. **Money that grows.** Compound interest, start-early (time is the one asset an 18-year-old has), index-over-stock-picking, the employer match (free money) - and the inoculation: active trading / meme-stocks / crypto are the casino, not investing. Do-it: model your own compound-growth curve; identify a real index fund and the match on a real (or sample) offer.
4. **Health coverage.** How you are covered the day you leave, how to get covered (employer, ACA marketplace, Medicaid, a parent's plan to 26); decode premium / deductible / copay / out-of-pocket / network; urgent care vs ER. Do-it: walk the real marketplace and price a plan; map your own path.
5. **Renting.** Read a real lease line by line; tenant rights vs responsibilities; the first+last+deposit wall; renters insurance exists and is cheap. Do-it: read a real lease and flag the traps.
6. **Navigating officialdom (the paper system).** *(Renamed from "the documents that are you" - a person is not their papers; that is the state's framing, and it wounds the undocumented, foster, refugee, and trans learner. - Kira, Tier 1.)* Know how the document system works and how to navigate it at arm's length: what a birth certificate / Social Security card / ID / passport are *for*, and how the replacement/application processes work, generically. **No status disclosure, ever** - this is the most protected decline surface in the strand, and it carries **no Socratic mirror** (the honest reason for declining might be a legal status the guide must not know or infer). Build the *actually-useful* version for the learner the old framing wounded: know-your-rights, spotting notario / immigration-help scams (pair with Spot-the-Trap). Do-it (own-situation only, never required): navigate one real official process you actually need.
7. **Spot the trap.** Recognize phishing, subscription cancel-traps, predatory lending, get-rich-quick schemes, and identity theft - and know what to do when hit. *(Evoked's own home turf.)* Do-it: dissect a real phishing email and a real cancel-flow; know the fraud-response steps.
8. **The first-job paperwork & your rights.** Fill a real W-4 (and know the I-9, direct deposit); at-will employment and your basic workplace rights; read a job offer for total comp and the match; and **know your worth - ask for fair pay, negotiate without shame** (Rom - anti-extraction is not anti-prosperity; the counterweight to a wallet you're afraid to fill). Do-it: complete a real W-4; decode a real or sample offer; role-play a real pay negotiation.

**The connective tissue (not a ninth item, the muscle under all eight):** *make one real call to a bureaucracy* - a benefits line, the DMV, an insurer - and get an answer. That single experience dissolves the terror-of-the-form for good, and builds the meta-disposition the whole strand is really teaching: a form is just a form; you can call and ask; you can find the right person before you sign; asking for help is not shame.

## Deliberately NOT on the Floor (the judgment calls)

- **Interpersonal growth / relationships / confidence / goal-setting** - off the Floor on purpose; it is the *entire three-year spine* of the Launch Pad (the Socratic, leadership, Council, Vineyard arc). Putting it on the admin Floor would both duplicate the core work and shrink something huge into a checkbox.
- **FAFSA / college enrollment** - universal *only if college-bound*; fails the "regardless of path" bar. Stays just-in-time (calendar-anchored, October).
- **Active trading / stock-picking / crypto, car-buying, entrepreneurship/LLC, travel, home-buying** - path-dependent or self-triggering; genuinely just-in-time, never Floor.
- **Government workings (civics)** - "how government is structured" is knowledge-delivery; the *practical* half (applying, the documents, the real call) is already on the Floor. See the enrichment strand below.

## The just-in-time reference library (the existing courses, reframed)

The dark-shipped Compass Life Skills and Financial Literacy courses are **not** thrown out and **not** something a learner sits through. They become the **reference manual the learner opens the moment they need it**, standing beside the real task. The strand is the doing-with-a-guide; the courses are the how-to you reach for mid-doing. This gives the existing build its right role and wastes nothing.

## Enrichment & path-dependent strands (real, valued - just not mandatory)

These run purely just-in-time / by interest, never as a Floor:

- **Grants & the shame-free ask.** Not Floor (path-dependent), but elevated above ordinary enrichment because it teaches the exact opposite disposition to predatory lending: *legitimate free money exists and you are allowed to ask for it.* Real do: write and submit one real scholarship or small-grant application. **Curriculum-lived:** the guide is, in real life, running Evoked's own grant search - modeling a real grant hunt is the sibling of the toddler-in-orbit lesson.
- **Government / civics, the reachable kind.** Anchored on the truth that *local government touches your life most and is the most reachable.* Real do: contact a real representative about a real issue, or sit in on one real local meeting (city council, school board). Where-your-taxes-go and local-vs-state-vs-federal ride along as context, not as a lecture.
- **The wider world of work - jobs you have never heard of.** Deliberate mind-opening: you cannot choose a path you do not know exists. Surface unusual, obscure, and unexpected careers (the person whose whole job is a thing no one at the table knew was a job). Pairs directly with S5's guest speakers - the most mind-opening visitor is rarely the doctor. Real do: each learner researches one genuinely surprising career and brings it to the cohort; the guest-speaker roster deliberately includes at least one unexpected vocation. This is joy-adjacent (Vineyard-flavored) - wonder at how many ways there are to make a life.
- **Capability & resilience (tech and signal).** The confidence-building, demystifying cluster: **keeping your technology updated** (why updates matter - it is mostly *security*; how to update devices and software) - pairs with Spot-the-trap; **the command line** (basic terminal use - the computer stops being a black box and becomes a tool you command); and **radio** (how it works, emergency and off-grid communication when the infrastructure fails - also a perfect doorway into the wider-world-of-work). Real do: run a real update on your own device and say what it patched; run five real terminal commands; make a real contact on a radio (and meet what an amateur-radio license is).
- **The rest, on demand:** FAFSA, car-buying, investing beyond the basics, entrepreneurship/LLC, travel logistics, home-buying - opened the moment a learner's real life reaches for them.

## The cross-year sequencing (the exposure-days, distributed)

Every item gets a light exposure-touch on a learner-day somewhere across the three years, so nothing is met for the first time at the launch cliff; the deeper do-it-for-real dive is then pulled just-in-time. Placement principle: earlier years get what a 15-16-year-old can act on now, the foundational, and the mind-opening; later years get the launch-critical and the legally-adult. Items that share a day are paired.

**The early-trigger engine (so "across three years" is real, not a launch-year cram):** the **Studio Commons** - a small, supportive shared-money practice that manufactures a real pay stub, W-4, and budget years before the outside world hands them one. *Ring-1 fix (2026-08-28): the Commons is cut to its smallest-true-thing and its launch moved to Year 2 (The Forge) - not the newborn-year Origin (see the section below). Origin now carries only the simplest real-artifact money touches (read a real pay stub, hold a real account); the shared practice begins in the Forge.*

| Year | Exposure-days (paired) | Why here |
|---|---|---|
| **Origin (Y1, ~15-16)** | Paycheck + banking (simplest real-artifact touches only - read a pay stub, hold an account); Navigating officialdom; Spot-the-trap + keep-your-tech-updated; Wider-world-of-work + the command line | What a 15-16-year-old can do *now*, the foundational money habits, digital hygiene, and mind-opening - fits Origin's "who am I / what's possible" |
| **The Forge (Y2, ~16-17)** | **The Studio Commons launches here** (fund + pay-stub/W-4 machinery); Money-that-grows (fits S5 "The Long Game"); Taxes (tax season, calendar-anchored); Grants + reachable civics; First-job paperwork + rights; Radio (fits the expeditions/hands-on) | The long-game and outward-turned items - funding, systems, the working world - fit The Forge's "what is worth solving"; and by Year 2 the founding cohort has a year of trust and the child is in Tots |
| **The Helm (Y3, ~17-18)** | Health coverage (aging-off transition); Renting (lease, tenant rights); the **launch-year sweep** + the decline-with-reasoning conversations; deeper dives on anything not yet done for real | The launch-critical, legally-adult, threshold items - fit The Helm's "the hard call, and loving the life around it" |

**The pairings** (items that share a learner-day because they share a muscle): Navigating officialdom + the bureaucracy call (dealing with the paper system); Spot-the-trap + tech-updating (digital hygiene/security); Money-that-grows + the first-job match (the 401k bridges them); Taxes + paycheck (your money and the government's cut); Grants + civics (the systems that fund and serve you); Wider-world-of-work + terminal + radio (the expand-what's-possible/capability cluster).

*Reconciling the year plans (open item):* this table is the strand's own placement; the three year-plan docs can cross-reference it rather than each re-listing the items. Editing the placement into each year plan (Origin/Forge/Helm) is a later, optional pass.

## The Studio Commons - the supportive micro-economy (support, not destroy)

> **RING-1 LEADERSHIP FIX (2026-08-28) - read this first; it governs the section below.**
> The most-converged change in the entire review (7 PDC + 8 SSC + 9 COC voices; ratified by leadership): **cut the Commons to its smallest-true-thing, and move its launch to Year 2 (The Forge).**
> - **What runs (smallest-true-thing, Year 2):** *Layer 1 - the pooled Commons Fund* (shared, stewarded) and *Layer 2 - the flat civic-role stipend* (the pay-stub / W-4 / year-end-tax machinery, real-artifact rung). These teach the money machinery and carry no individual-output signal, so they cannot drift to productivity-as-worth (Data's stability test). **The Fund is never fed by learner or family contribution**, and no shared good is ever gated on what a learner can put in (Kira - class floor).
> - **What becomes OPTIONAL, interest-pulled deepening a *thriving* cohort grows toward - never pre-built by the guide:** *Layer 3 - the cooperative Micro-Venture*, the learner-authored Charter, the cross-cohort inheritance, and the outward-share. "A commons a cohort *asks* to build is a commons; one the adults pre-assemble is an allowance with ceremony" (Guinan). The reason (Spock's tell): a structure that needed ~a dozen guards across four versions to stop teaching its own anti-lesson was fighting its own gravity - so we keep the part whose natural attractor *is* its purpose, and let the rest be pulled, not pushed.
> - **The detailed design below is retained as the reference for those optional deepenings** - what a cohort builds toward *if and when they reach for it*, with all its guards intact. It is no longer the Year-1 default.

The micro-economy is the engine that makes "across three years" real, and it carries the strand's sharpest values trap: a careless "studio economy" teaches money-competence *and* quietly teaches "my worth is my output." That is the exact anti-lesson - productivity-as-worth, refusal #5. The design below is built to teach the machinery of money while teaching the opposite of extraction: that an economy can run on **stewardship and reciprocity**, and that a person's worth is never their earnings.

**The governing principle:** money is *a skill the crew practices together* - held lightly, the way a wrench is a tool for plumbing - never a *scoreboard that ranks its members* and never a *sacred shared object that defines the crew.* Every design choice serves that line. (Beverly's reframe: centering money as "the shared thing we steward" can quietly make money *more* central to identity, not less; the Commons is a laboratory for a skill, not a shrine.)

Three layers:

1. **The Commons Fund (shared, stewarded - the heart).** A real pooled fund the cohort stewards for shared goods: expeditions, materials, the Table, guest-speaker honoraria, a community-benefit project. Learners rotate *real* financial roles - **Treasurer** (track and reconcile the budget), **Grant-writer** (write for real money - ties to the grants strand), **Purchaser** (make real buys, read invoices/receipts), **Allocator-facilitator** (leads the deliberation on how to spend - ties to the Council/facilitation muscle). Teaches budgeting, reconciliation, real money-handling, and *allocation-as-governance* - with no individual earnings and no ranking. The money serves the crew.

2. **The Role Stipend (equal, for the machinery - the trigger).** To generate the real pay stub + W-4 + year-end tax experience, whoever holds a rotating studio role receives a **small, equal, flat stipend**. Framed out loud as a **civic-role stipend, not a wage for output** (Kasidy) - the way a juror or a board member is paid for taking a turn carrying a shared duty, not for how much they produce. That framing dissolves the "pay decoupled from work" distortion (flatness with a reason a teenager can articulate, not a rule imposed). **Rotation is real, short, and guaranteed-complete** (Beverly, Kasidy, Naomi): weeks not months, every learner reaches every role over time - because flat pay sitting on *unequal role-access* is the scoreboard sneaking back through the side door. The point is the *machinery*, never the amount, never a comparison.

3. **The Micro-Venture (optional, cooperative - the deeper lesson).** If the cohort wants to grow the Commons Fund, they may run a real *cooperative* venture (a service, a product, a stall) - cooperatively owned, proceeds to the Commons (non-proportional - no one's share tracks who "sold most"), not to individuals. Teaches revenue and cooperative economics - the anti-extraction story lived, not lectured. Optional, interest-driven. **Four guards (Kasidy, Rom, Amanda, Beverly):**
   - **Plan the free-rider grievance IN, not out.** Unequal effort with equal proceeds *will* surface ("I did all the work"); that is the lesson, not the bug. Handle it as a *facilitated deliberation* (graduated response, the Council muscle), never suppressed and never resolved with a who-did-most scoreboard.
   - **Cooperative for real, not in name.** Ownership must be *exercised* (collective choice on what the venture is, what it charges, where proceeds go), not one or two leaders deciding while the rest rubber-stamp.
   - **The sovereignty check, pointed outward (Rom - "the most Evoked move, currently absent").** Before the stall opens, the cohort runs their own venture through a kid-scaled sovereignty check: "would we be proud to explain this to the person who bought it, and to whoever supplied us?" No upsells, no dark-pattern pricing, no cutting a corner on a supplier. Cooperation-among-us must not become extraction-toward-them.
   - **Belonging is never contingent on the venture succeeding (Beverly).** If it underperforms, the frame is "cooperative economics is hard and learning includes it not working" - never "we're the cohort that couldn't." A group-worth wound is a belonging wound, and belonging is a floor, not a reward.

**The values guardrails (support-not-destroy), explicit:**
- **Flat, equal stipends; a shared fund.** No individual earnings ranked against each other; no productivity-as-worth.
- **You are not your role, and you are not your cohort's revenue (Beverly).** Named as loudly and as often as "money is a tool" - because worth leaks through the back doors flat pay doesn't cover: role-status, "best steward" esteem, venture success/failure. Disown all three out loud.
- **Money is a tool, not a verdict** - and *money is also good, and earning is honorable (Rom).* Anti-extraction is not anti-prosperity. A person may want things, be ambitious, hold what they earn without shame, and ask plainly for fair pay. The sin is never having or wanting money - it is taking it from someone who did not freely give it. (Without this half, the design quietly teaches that wanting money is suspect, which produces adults who are easy to underpay. Lean on the Grants strand - the honorable individual ask - as the counterweight, and add "know your worth / negotiate without shame" to the first-job work.)
- **No "best steward."** Stewardship is *witnessed, never scored, never singled out for comparative praise* (Beverly, Naomi) - praise is the vector productivity-as-worth rides in on once surveillance is banned. And *unconditional regard on failure is engineered, not hoped for:* a learner who fumbles a role is met with the same warmth as one who excels, visibly, so the room learns worth survives a bad day. That felt experience - not the poster - is what makes "money is not a verdict" true.
- **Monitoring is transparency-among-peers, never surveillance-by-authority (Amanda).** The books are open to the whole cohort; the *resource* is monitored, not the person. Nothing is reported up to the guide as a contribution-score.
- **Teach the alternative economic story - and name all three (Amanda).** Market (worth = output), charity (one-directional; giver-over-receiver), mutual aid (all are both givers and receivers). Let the cohort *notice* they chose mutual aid over the other two; the noticing is the lesson. Frame the community-benefit spend as *reciprocity, not donation* - the deepest generosity still receives.
- **Stewardship over accumulation** - the win is tending the shared thing well, not accumulating personally. The six refusals hold: no ranking, no productivity-as-worth, no surveillance of who "contributed most."

**What it manufactures (the whole point - real do-it-for-real, early):** a real pay stub (item 1), a real W-4 (item 8), a real budget (items 1-2), real year-end taxes (item 1), real grant-writing (grants strand), real allocation/governance (civics + the Council muscle), real invoicing/purchasing - and, optionally, real cooperative revenue.

**The honest operational tension (open item):** *real* payroll for tiny stipends is genuine admin burden for a solo guide (withholding, filing). Two ways to hold it: run the pay-stub/W-4/tax learning on the **real-artifact rung** (the studio "runs payroll" as a real-mechanics exercise producing real forms, without full employer-payroll liability), and put **real dollars** where it is feasible (the Commons Fund, the cooperative venture). The do-it-for-real ladder already licenses this - the learning is real even where the dollars are simulated.

### From pot to commons - the learner-authored Charter (Amanda)

A pooled fund the adults set the rules for is an allowance with rotating clerks. A *commons* is a community that makes its own rules for a shared resource and holds itself to them. The single change that converts one into the other: the cohort **writes (and annually amends) its own short Commons Charter** - membership, how we decide to spend, what happens when a role isn't held (graduated, learner-designed sanctions), how we handle a fight about money, and how we amend the rules. Learner-authored, short, living; the guide facilitates, never dictates (the guide-has-no-veto rule extends here). **The charter-writing is the curriculum.** Decisions run **consent-based** (sociocratic - "can anyone *not* live with this?") rather than majority-vote, which is the specific antidote to capture-by-the-loud: the quiet learner's objection weighs the same as the loud learner's enthusiasm.

**Allocation-as-governance is the spine, not a parenthetical (Amanda).** Deciding together how to steward a shared resource is the founding civic skill - the town meeting, the co-op, the union hall. Wire Fund allocation into the existing **Council** as a live, real-money deliberation. Deliberation-without-stakes is a seminar; deliberation over a real shared resource is citizenship. Two lessons from one pot: the *machinery* of money rides the stipend layer; the *civics* of the commons rides the Fund layer.

### The outward edge and the inheritance (Kasidy, Amanda, Naomi)

As drawn, the Fund only flows *inward* (the crew funds the crew) - which is a well-governed household budget, not yet reciprocity. Two additions make the gift real:
- **An outward share.** Promote "community-benefit" from a buried spend-category to a structural, crew-decided *fixed fraction that flows out* to something the crew does not benefit from - framed as *reciprocity* (done *with* a community, receiving a relationship back), never one-directional charity. That is the moment a teenager feels the difference between spending and giving.
- **The cross-cohort inheritance.** Decide it, do not default it: the Commons **inherits across years** - each cohort receives a tended fund, grows it, and hands it forward (Origin leaves for next Origin; Helm reciprocates to Forge). This completes the gift cycle across people the giver will never meet, and turns a three-year exercise into a real commons with a past and a future. (Real cost to weigh: what if a cohort depletes it? - a Charter question for the cohorts.)
- **Both outward flows carry a ritual (Kasidy/Neelix)**, or they degrade into ledger lines - the handing-forward and the community gift are *felt*, not filed.

### Prepare them for the real market, not only the garden (Rom)

A learner who only ever experiences a warm, flat, cooperative commons walks into an at-will, sometimes-extractive market with a map of a country that does not exist. So the Commons models the better economy **and rehearses the real one, named as two different games.** Stage one guided encounter with a *non-cooperative* counterparty - a supplier who won't budge, a predatory contract for the venture to read and refuse, a manipulative sales flow to spot - with the guide beside them (the strand's own thesis). Then name it plainly: *"The Commons is how we choose to run our economy. Out there, most are not run this way. Your job is not to be shocked - it is to survive it with your sovereignty intact, and to build the better kind wherever you can."*

### The vulnerable learners (Beverly - non-negotiable)

- **The financially-strained learner.** Money-as-shared-sacred-object can activate a teenager whose family cannot make rent; and the small stipend that is a *toy* to an affluent learner may be a *lifeline* to a poor one - which reintroduces the very meaning-differential flat pay was meant to erase. So the **stipend stays on the real-artifact rung (simulated dollars, real forms), never real income a learner relies on**; real dollars live only in the collective Fund and venture. A quiet, private opt-down exists. "Money is just a tool" is never said carelessly to a room that may hold someone for whom it has never once been just a tool.
- **The productivity-bound learner.** The perfectionist whose worth already requires proof will read the Commons as a fresh, morally-sanctioned arena to perform in (flawless stewardship, grants that land). Their tell is *over*-engagement, not disengagement. Screen for them before the Commons runs; for them it is watched, not just offered (Salus + Jake + Beverly define the watch-fors); the therapeutic move is witness-before-fix and making it explicitly safe to be an unremarkable steward.

### The drift watch (Naomi - the Commons is the one drift-capable object)

## The readiness map (completeness without a deadline)

The honest tension: pure just-in-time risks a learner launching having never triggered "taxes" or "health coverage." The fix is **not** a graded checklist (that violates the six refusals). Instead:

- A **learner-owned readiness map** - a menu of the real-world tasks (Floor + just-in-time + enrichment), that the learner drives as life triggers them. Lives in the Compass Life Skills tab; feeds the Growth Record; the Captain's Log holds the reflection. It is a *map they own*, not a compliance tracker the guide checks off - and under the v0.2 model it records **reasoned choices** (done / did-with-a-guide / declined-because-___), not pass/fail.
- The **launch-year sweep** confirms every Floor item has been *met at the exposure layer* and invites the deeper dive, gently ("what is still unopened?"), never as an audit, a deadline, or a grade. A learner may decline a dive - with their reasoning - and that reasoned "no" is honored. That is how sovereignty and a real floor coexist: the floor guarantees the *exposure and the offer*, the learner owns the *depth.*

## Keeping it current (the maintenance problem, turned into a lesson)

This is the one strand whose content rots - tax forms, ACA rules, the FAFSA (mid-overhaul), marketplace URLs, benefit thresholds. A "do it for real" strand that teaches last year's form is worse than none, because it teaches *wrong* with false confidence. Two defenses, and the second is the better one:

- **A named currency owner + an annual sweep.** Someone owns a check of the do-it-for-real kits before each tax/FAFSA/enrollment season. A learner cannot be the only line of defense on whether the form is right.
- **The learner-update-check, as a taught skill (Europa).** When an item is presented, the learner runs an *update-check* - "is this form / rule / number still current, and how would I verify it?" This does double duty: it keeps the content honest *and* teaches the meta-skill of verifying currency - which is exactly the discipline Evoked holds for itself (research-currency, before you rely on a claim). Learning to check whether your information is current - and learning to update your own technology - are life skills in their own right, not overhead.

The currency-check argues for **keeping the Floor small (eight) and resisting the urge to grow it** - every added item is another thing that rots.

## Six-refusals compliance

No grades, no ranking, no banking-model deposit, no external deadline, no productivity-as-worth, no surveillance. The Floor is offered as care; the readiness map is learner-owned; the sweep is an invitation, not an audit. The one thing asked of everyone is *exposure* (learn what each thing is) and, if declining the deeper dive, a *reasoned why* - which is an exercise of judgment, not a compliance gate. A learner who reasons their way to "not this, not now, and here is why" has exercised sovereignty exactly as designed.
