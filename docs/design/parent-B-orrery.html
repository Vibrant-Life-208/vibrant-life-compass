VIBRANT LIFE  ·  BOISE  ·  AGES 16 TO 18

We promise the conditions.
Never the outcome.

What a parent asks first about the Launch Pad, answered in print: what the week is, what we refuse to do, what one guide can and cannot hold, and how you leave.

What it is

A three-year studio at Vibrant Life, Boise, for learners who are 16 by the August they start. Not a high school. One cohort, one guide, one room, and a plan for all three years that is printed and readable by the learner.

Year one asks Who am I? Year two asks What is worth solving? Year three asks What do I do when there is no easy answer? A learner may join at any year. The story is in progress, never behind.

The week

MonThe learner's own morning. Reading, claimed credit work, their project. The guide is in the building, in another room, and comes to them around noon.
TueGuide morning, 9:00 to 1:00. Walk-in, settle, the work of the day, lunch at the Table, hands-on work, a short close.
WedThe learner's own morning, as Monday.
ThuGuide morning, the same shape as Tuesday, every time.
FriEmpty. Not earned, not filled, not on call.

Seven sessions a year with short breaks between, two weeks at winter, and three loose weeks in May. Dates are projected from this year's Vibrant Life calendar; the school sets the real ones.

What we refuse to do

  • grades, and no ranking of one learner against another.
  • deadlines the learner didn't set themselves.
  • reading the learner's Log. It is theirs, it leaves with them, and no copy is kept.
  • showing a learner's work, face, or story in anything we publish.
  • promising you who your child will be in three years. We can tell you what the mornings are. We cannot tell you what they will make of them, and we will not pretend to.
  • filling Friday.

These are not the small print. They are the design. A teenager can read every page of the plan and find nothing we hid from them.

What gets written down

A Growth Record the learner owns: what they did, in their words and the guide's, with nothing graded. Claimed high-school credit is tracked so it can be shown. No college yet recognizes the record on its own; the dual-enrollment bridge is something we will work on with you and tell you honestly where it stands.

What one guide can hold

It is one guide, Erin Stanley, and no aide. On guide mornings her baby is in the room, usually in a carrier. Some mornings she will be holding a child while you talk. The room holds a handful of learners, and when it is full it is full; we will say so plainly rather than keep a list warm.

The guide is a coach, not a therapist. Every learner is given, in print, the name of one adult outside the room they can go to, and 988, which is free, all hours, call or text. Over a break the guide is not reachable. That is her rest too, and the plan says so.

How you leave

Any time. The way out is written down before anyone starts, and leaving costs nothing between us. A learner who says no, or not yet, is never told they are behind.

What to do next

Ask for the printed books; there are three, one per year, and the learner should read them too. Come sit in the room one evening, do the thirty-second settle yourselves, eat something at the Table, and hear the refusals read aloud. Then decide at home.
