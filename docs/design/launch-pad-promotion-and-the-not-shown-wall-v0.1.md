## 0. The posture, in one line

**Invitation, not persuasion.** We recruit on the design and the refusals. We show the room, never the child. We state the limit as a limit, never as a lure. We promise the conditions, never the outcome.

The Launch Pad is a Vibrant Life studio. It is promoted by Vibrant Life and by Erin Stanley as its guide. No Evoked branding, no fleet names, no company story rides on it.

---

## 1. The "Deliberately NOT Shown" wall

The outreach sibling of "deliberately NOT built" (three-year spec, Ring-2 #8). Written now because the pilot morning is the most photogenic, most shareable moment the program will ever produce, and it happens before any wall exists. Restraint that lives only in a reviewer's head is a good intention waiting for a proud afternoon to overwrite it.

**Never in any outreach, post, page, poster, letter, talk, or conversation:**

1. **Interior work as content.** Not a dream from a 100-Dreams list, not a line from a Log, not a family story, not a Council deliberation, not a closing-practice answer. The interior is not the program's to show.
2. **Transformation as proof.** No before/after. No "she came in shy and left a leader." No arc told about a real learner.
3. **A learner's story, image, voice, or likeness**, except under a standard that is adult-only in decision, learner-owned, withdrawable at any time, owes no favor, and contains no interior content. Default: none. A minor cannot consent to indefinite public representation of their interior, and **a parent's consent is not the child's.**
4. **Outcome promises or alumni metrics.** No "graduates go on to." No numbers about learners, ever.
5. **A founder story whose emotional engine is a specific child's formation.** Erin may say why she built this. She may not make her son, or any child, the reason on a page.
6. **Re-contacting a graduate for a testimonial.** The exit is empty. That includes the exit into marketing.
7. **Urgency and scarcity.** "Only seven seats" is a fact when a family asks and a lever when it leads. It never leads. No deadlines dressed as excitement, no "spots are filling," no countdown.
8. **The pilot morning.** No photos, no write-up, no post. It is a mirror for the guide, not a launch event.
9. **Borrowed visual language of struggle or transformation** (protest typography, journey-to-triumph imagery). The poster is a star map and a cycle. It stays that.
10. **AI-generated imagery of children or of the room.** Photographs of the empty studio, hand drawings, and the existing brass-line design are the visual vocabulary.

**What may be shown, freely:** the design (the three lenses, the seven sessions, the weekly rhythm); the refusals, as the feature they are; the room, empty; the guide, as themselves; the honest limits; the price and the exit; the printed books, because a teenager can read every page of them.

## 2. Who we invite, in order

1. **Current Vibrant Life families whose learner turns 16 by the 2027 cutoff.** The Compass already plants this intention: the Adventure-to-Launch-Pad pitch page ("will you have turned 16 by the cutoff? want to start getting ready?") is built and gated on the ratified thresholds. Turning it on is a code decision for the studio, not a promotion decision, and it is the one in-app door.
2. **Vibrant Life siblings and alumni families.** They already know what the school refuses.
3. **Jenna and Wes's community**, at their cadence and with their word, not ours.
4. **Boise's plural town**, through relationships rather than reach: the homeschool and co-op networks, the library (Boothby's sovereign research layer), the CWI/BSU dual-enrollment office (the credential bridge a family will ask about first). Each is a conversation, not a campaign.
5. **Never:** paid ads, cold lists, the Evoked audience, social posts that could be read as recruitment of strangers' children.

Target 5-6, ceiling 7 (year plan v0.3). If the first two circles fill the room, the third and fourth are relationships kept for the mixed-cycle joiner, not a second round of recruiting.

## 3. What we show

- **The room.** One evening, "come see the room": families sit in the studio, do the thirty-second settle themselves, play one choice game, eat something at the Table, and hear the refusals read aloud. No learners on display. A low-sensory seat and a written version of everything said are available in the room, not on request.
- **The printed books.** The three lesson plan books (Origin, Forge, Helm) are the promotion. A parent reads what their kid will do; a teenager reads the same pages. Nothing is hidden, so nothing needs to be sold.
- **The poster and the one-pager.** The turning cycle, the three questions, the Captain's Log. Already made.
- **The honest limits, out loud and in print:** one guide, an infant worn on guide days, no aide; self-managed Monday and Wednesday mornings; a Growth Record no institution yet recognizes and the dual-enrollment bridge we will help build; the price; how you leave.
- **The door:** the first conversation, the access statement, the fit signs, published before anyone applies.

## 4. The sentences

**Use freely**
- "A three-year studio for learners aged 16 to 18. Not a high school."
- "Two mornings a week with a guide. Two mornings the learner runs. Friday is never filled."
- "No grades. No ranking. No deadlines you didn't set. Your Log is yours and no one reads it."
- "Here is what one guide can hold, and here is what they can't."
- "You can leave any time. Here is exactly how."
- "The room has seven chairs this year." (When asked. Never as the opener.)
- "Come sit in the room and decide for yourself."

**Never**
- "Transform," "unlock," "unleash," "the leader your child was meant to be."
- "Limited spots," "applications closing," "don't miss."
- "Our graduates," "results," any number about a learner.
- "Erin built this for her son." (Her reasons are hers to say in person; not on a page.)
- Any sentence that makes a family feel they are behind if they say no.

## 5. The invitation letter (draft, Erin's voice, to a family that already knows Vibrant Life)

> Hello,
>
> Next August, Vibrant Life opens a studio for learners who will be 16 by then: the Launch Pad. It is three years long. The first year asks one question, *Who am I?* The second asks *What is worth solving?* The third asks *What do I do when there is no easy answer?* I'll be the guide.
>
> It is not a high school. There are no grades, no rankings, and no deadlines the learner didn't set. Two mornings a week we work together; two mornings the learners run themselves; Friday stays empty on purpose. Each learner keeps a Log that nobody reads but them.
>
> I want to be plain about the limits. It is one guide, and I will have a baby with me on guide days. There is no aide. The record a learner leaves with is not a transcript any college yet recognizes; we will work on the dual-enrollment bridge together, and I'll tell you honestly where that stands. The room holds about six learners.
>
> I've printed the whole plan for all three years, written so your kid can read every page. If you'd like a copy, say so. If you'd like to come sit in the room one evening and try a morning yourselves, I'll set a date. If you'd rather not, nothing changes between us.
>
> Erin

*(Price, dates, and the exit procedure ride on the Family note that goes with this letter; the letter never carries them alone.)*

## 8. Deliberately NOT in this plan

No campaign. No funnel. No newsletter list built from families. No tracking of who opened what. No social media recruitment of minors. No paid reach. No outcome language. No child on a page.

---

*Show the room. Print the books. Say the limits. Then let the family decide.*
