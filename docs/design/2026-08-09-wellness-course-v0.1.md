## The spine (binding - from the research)

- Aim is a **capacity, not a product or an aesthetic**: to *tend your body and mind with care,* mostly
  through simple, free, durable practices. Never an optimization project, never a number, never a look.
- **Connection is central.** The single strongest predictor of wellbeing - across a lifetime (Harvard
  Study) and inside adolescence (where it out-predicted sleep, activity, nutrition, and mindfulness).
  Beneficence is native: you tend yourself so you can live fully and be present for others, and being
  with others is itself core to being well.
- **Self-compassion, not self-optimization** (large protective effect in adolescents; a better target
  than self-esteem). The inner skill is kindness to yourself, not a better score of yourself.
- **The firewall** (the honest test): does a wellness message build the capacity to tend yourself, or
  the feeling that you are a *project that is never finished* - never thin/clean/optimized enough? The
  tell is who it centers: your actual flourishing and your people, or your appearance, metrics, and
  consumption.
- **Experiential.** Every step is a doing, same-day, needing nothing you don't already have.

## Safety (binding - this course is trauma-adjacent)

Adolescent wellness borders diet culture, body image, disordered eating, orthorexia, and self-harm. The
AED ("First, Do No Harm") and AAP (2016) consensus is built into every step:
- **Focus on behaviors and care, never on weight, dieting, appearance, or tracking.** No counting, no
  measuring, no "good/bad food," no weigh-ins. One step actively has the learner *drop* a number.
- **Build protective factors:** body appreciation (what your body lets you *do*), self-compassion,
  connection, family meals.
- **Owed review:** Salus/Bashir + Jake developmental pass is load-bearing here, more than for the other
  three courses. Do not ship to a real child before it. Separate from, and does not touch, the still-owed
  Wave-2 young-learner safety gate (`agents/meetings/2026/08/2026-08-04-young-learner-safety-review-wave2.md`).

## The arc (Tend -> Be Kind -> Connect)

Tending yourself and the wellness industry start at the same door: your body and mind, and the wish to
feel well. The whole ethics is what you do there - tend, or treat yourself as a thing to fix. **Tend**
(the free body basics, done kindly) -> **Be Kind** (self-compassion + the firewall) -> **Connect** (the
strongest thing for a good life is people). The honest test runs through all three: *tending yourself, or
fixing a project that never finishes?*

---

## Stage 1 - TEND  ·  Care for your body with small, real, free things.

The durable basics - sleep, moving, being outside, eating - done as care, not as a scoreboard. Nothing to
buy, nothing to count.

1. **Sleep one night well.** Pick a real wind-down tonight - screens down early, lights low - and protect
   your sleep. (Sleep is one of the few things strongly tied to feeling better.)
2. **Move because it feels good.** Do one kind of moving you actually enjoy - a walk, dancing, a bike, a
   game - for how it feels, not to burn anything or hit a number.
3. **Get outside.** Spend a little time in some green or open air today. (Being outdoors helps - modestly,
   but really.)
4. **Eat something that nourishes you.** Make or choose one thing that fuels you - no "good" or "bad"
   food, no counting, no earning it. Food is care, not a scoreboard.

## Stage 2 - BE KIND  ·  Treat yourself like someone you're helping, not a project to fix.

The inner skill of wellbeing is kindness to yourself - which protects you more reliably than chasing a
better opinion of yourself. This stage holds the firewall.

1. **Talk to yourself like a friend.** Catch one harsh thing you say to yourself, and say instead what
   you'd tell a friend who felt that way. (Self-kindness protects wellbeing more than chasing self-esteem.)
2. **Rest your attention.** Take a few real minutes doing nothing to fix and nothing to scroll - just
   breathe, notice, be. Rest isn't laziness.
3. **The honest test (firewall).** Notice one "wellness" message aimed at you - an ad, an influencer, an
   app. Ask: is this helping me tend myself, or telling me I'm a project that's never fixed - never
   thin/clean/optimized enough? Name the sell.
4. **Drop one number.** Step away from one thing you track or measure about your body for a day, and
   notice how your body actually feels. Your body's signals, not a metric.

## Stage 3 - CONNECT  ·  The strongest thing for a good life is people.

The single strongest predictor of wellbeing, over a whole life and inside adolescence, is the quality of
your relationships. This is where wellness turns outward.

1. **Reach one person.** Message or sit with someone you like, today. If today is too much, that's not a
   failure - wanting to counts, and there's always another day. (Connection predicts wellbeing more
   strongly than sleep, food, or exercise.) *(SSC pre-review fix W-1, 2026-08-10: the reassurance sits
   beside the pressure, not in fine print - "reach one person today" must not read as a measure-of-failure
   for an isolated teen who can't.)*
2. **Tend one relationship.** Do one small thing for a bond that matters - listen fully, say thanks, show
   up. (It's the quality, not the count, that matters.)
3. **Ask for or offer help.** Let someone help you with one thing, or help someone with theirs. Both ends
   of care are wellbeing.
4. **Belong to something.** Show up once, when you're ready, for a group or place you're part of. Being
   present for others is part of being well yourself. *(SSC pre-review fix W-1, 2026-08-10: "when you're
   ready" so belonging is an open door, not a due date.)*

## Notes for the build

- **Register.** v0.1 is the 12-18 register, matching CLIMB and the other Life Skills courses. The 8-11
  young version is Wave-2 - not written here, not shipped silently. At younger ages the same arc holds but
  the language softens (tend, be kind, be with people); no tracking/weight language at any age, ever.
- **No tracking, no weight, no metrics - by design.** The course never asks a learner to count, weigh,
  measure, or optimize their body. Step 2.4 has them *drop* a number.

  Skills -> Wellness surface, distinct from the onboarding WOOP. Read-only guidance; the WOOP is the
