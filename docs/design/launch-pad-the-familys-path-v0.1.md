# The Family's Path - what a family and a learner receive, and in what order

**Status:** v0.1 working draft, authored 2026-09-26. A sequence and a gap map, not a new design. Everything here was decided in the three 8-voice sittings of 2026-09-24 and 2026-09-25; this document only puts the pieces in order and marks which ones do not exist yet.
**Companions:** `2027-2028-launch-pad-first-cohort-conversation-v0.1.md` (the door), `launch-pad-promotion-and-the-not-shown-wall-v0.1.md` (what may be shown), `launch-pad-the-ballast-and-open-days-v0.1.md` (the waiting learner), `launch-pad-STATUS-AND-HANDOFF.md` §5 (the owed writing list).

*Not convened as a fourth meeting. Sequencing decisions the room already made is execution, and a fourth sitting on the same program in three days would be the articulateness pattern both Cura and Naomi watch for. The standing conditions still govern every piece below: Chekov on access, Kira on plain language, Accord on the exhale, Comes on the walk.*

---

## Three rules that set the order

1. **The limits arrive before the invitation.** The access statement, the cost, the exit, and the removal policy are published before a family is ever asked to be interested. A family that reads them and steps away has been served well. Consent that hides its limits is not consent.
2. **Nothing arrives that assumes a yes not yet given.** No notebook before a seat. No Ballast before a place on the list. No Charter before a conversation.
3. **The learner gets their own copies.** Almost everything at the door is written to a parent. The learner chooses the mode of their own conversation, gives their own yes a week later, and is the person the three years happen to. They should have read things themselves, in their own hands, not filtered.

## What is missing, ranked

Seven of the gaps above are ordinary writing. Three of them block the door.

1. **The cost.** No Launch Pad document names a number, and the door has a hole in the middle without one. Jenna and Wes hold it.
2. **The access statement.** Named as owed in three consecutive sittings. Chekov's condition is binding: no conversation before it exists, because "not a fit" enforced without it is disability screening nobody decided to do.
3. **What happens if something happens to you.** A family committing a child to three years with a solo guide who is pregnant will ask this, and the honest answer today is a document that does not exist. The Successor Envelope is already P0-2, the first item on the handoff's founder list, to be authored while rested and co-signed. It is also the answer to the hardest question at the door.

Then, in rough order of how soon a family will want them: a day on a page; the year calendar; the learner-facing page; the FAQ with a named owner for the dual-enrollment clock; the three reply letters; the guide's card; the evening run sheet; the learner's refusals card.

## What would support the adventure, beyond the paperwork

Three things the design already names and has not built, which would do more for a family's confidence than any additional document.

- **The named outside adult, on paper.** One external, non-founder, non-parent, non-employee adult, reachable directly by the learner and the family, who can say a binding no the guide cannot overrule. It is already the single consolidated Ring-3 hook. A family reading that sentence in the Family note relaxes in a way no reassurance achieves.
- **The dual-enrollment bridge, with an owner and a clock.** The credential question is the one a parent loses sleep over, and the design says the window closes on a clock nobody owns. One phone call converts the FAQ's weakest answer into its strongest.
- **The Cohort Call, one page.** The consent primitive the cohort uses to decide the things that are theirs, including who sits in their Unveiling. It seats in Origin Session 3, and it is what makes "co-created" true rather than decorative.
