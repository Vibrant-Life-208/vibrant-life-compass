**One document for every way out, because a family asked at four different moments should not get four different answers.** It closes two gaps found on 2026-10-21: the pilot morning had no withdrawal path at all, and the FAQ could not answer what happens if a family moves mid-year.

**The principle underneath all four moments.** Leaving is a door, not a failure, and the cost of using it should never be higher than the cost of staying somewhere wrong. Where that principle meets an existing Vibrant Life policy, the conflict is named below rather than quietly resolved in either direction.

---

## 1. Before the pilot morning

From the Family note, in its exact words:

> **If you change your mind.** Any time before the morning, including the night before, text me one word: *out.* You do not owe me a reason and I will not ask for one. I will not bring it up again, then or later.

There is no meeting, no call, and no conversation about whether you are sure.

Quoted rather than paraphrased so the two documents cannot drift apart. If one changes, change both.

**Why it is this easy.** The first learners are the founder's child and this community's children, and a family who felt unable to decline on the phone will not decline in the room. The cheapest possible exit afterwards is the only honest correction for that.

---

## 2. After a yes, before the year starts

**The learner's yes is theirs to take back, at any point, without penalty.**

A seat is held on a learner's own yes, given alone, a week after the conversations. If they change their mind between then and the first day, tell the guide and that is the end of it. No fee, no meeting, no conversation about whether they are sure, and the seat passes to the list without comment.

**A family changing its mind is treated the same way**, with one difference: if your learner still wants to come and you no longer do, the guide will ask to hear that from your learner directly, once, with the second adult present. Not to change anyone's mind. Because a sixteen-year-old's yes was taken seriously on the way in and gets the same seriousness on the way out.

## 3. During the year

Here is where this document meets the school's existing policy, and the two do not sit flush.

**Vibrant Life's policy**, from the family playbook: *"We resolve issues through conversation first. We ask for two meetings with the guide and two with Jenna before withdrawing. If a transition is still right, the current month's tuition is prorated and refunded."*

**The Launch Pad's promise** is that your learner may refuse the structure itself, and that leaving costs nothing.

**The conflict, stated plainly.** Four required meetings is not nothing. For most families it is a reasonable ask and it does repair things that would otherwise break. For a family leaving because the room is harming their learner, or because of something they cannot say to the guide's face, it is four conversations with the person they are leaving.

**\[PENDING, Erin and Jenna\]** Which of these applies to a Launch Pad family. Three ways it could go, and this document will not choose for you:

- The school policy applies unchanged, and the Launch Pad says so out loud at the door rather than discovering it at the exit.
- The meetings are offered rather than required, and a family can decline them in one sentence.
- The meetings are required **except** where the learner says the room is unsafe for them, in which case the exit is immediate and the appeal path is not the guide.

Until this is settled, no Launch Pad document should say "you can leave any time" without saying what leaving involves.

**What is not in question:** nobody is kept by making leaving embarrassing, and no learner is asked to explain their family's decision to the room.

---

## 4. If the studio ends it

Removal is **a written, witnessed act with an appeal path that is not the guide.** It is never a conversation that a family finds out afterwards was the decision.

The agreements ladder is the one route to this and the whole of it is published: a Pause, a Stop, a Strike, then Step Away at three Strikes, with a real conversation long before anyone is near it and a way to re-apply the next year. A learner reaching Step Away is not back for the rest of the year. The count clears at the start of each year.

**Declining work is never on this ladder.** Refusing a quest, a prompt, a share or the whole structure cannot produce a Pause, and anyone who runs those two together has got it wrong.
