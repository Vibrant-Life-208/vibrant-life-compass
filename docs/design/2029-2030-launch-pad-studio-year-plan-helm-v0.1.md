## The convergent finding the plan is built on

The six-voice panel was unanimous in shape: **Europa's direction - make The Helm as honest and real as possible for 17/18-year-olds about to enter the real world - is correct and endorsed.** For near-adults three months from adulthood, a padded room would be its own quiet harm: a lie told at the threshold. Keep every word of the honesty.

And the honesty is safe only to the exact degree the *container* is built. Bashir's load-bearing sentence: *depth of material is safe in inverse proportion to how exposed the person is while meeting it.* The trauma floor is not the brake on honesty; it is the suspension that lets the vehicle take rough ground at speed. Jake: *honest-and-held transforms; honest-and-abandoned wounds - the whole verdict is that one word, held.* K'Ehleyr: *honest and real is a somatic promise, not only a curricular one.*

The gap is never the material. It is that the container, this year, is one solo guide holding the heaviest content in the arc. This plan grows the container.

## What The Helm is

*Lens: what do I do when there is no easy answer? Joy-flavor: expression & camaraderie. Anchor: "The first duty of every Starfleet officer is to the truth" / the Kobayashi Maru - the test was never how to win, but how to face a no-win scenario without losing yourself.*

The arc completes. Origin was **dreaming it** (who am I?); The Forge was **building it** (what is worth solving?); The Helm is **the hard call, and loving the life around it** (what do I do when there is no easy answer?). Self -> world -> the hard call. It is the year a young person learns to reason from principle under genuine uncertainty, live with a hard decision, lead peers, be mentored by the world's adults - and choose life *because* they have looked at hard things.

**Signature daily ritual:** the **Kobayashi Maru frame** (successor to Origin's choice games and The Forge's Impossible Question) - a no-win scenario faced without losing yourself; the practice of standing inside a dilemma that has no clean answer, long enough to reason from principle rather than reach for the exit.

**The Kobayashi Maru at full power (Kirk + Curzon).** The famous move - reprogram the simulator, beat the unbeatable test - is the bait, not the lesson. The deeper truth, for near-adults three months from the world: the test is not whether you can find the third option; it is who you are when there is *no* third option. Sometimes the freighter dies. So the year teaches *both halves*: the discernment to find the third door when it is real (Curzon's Third Chair, S7) **and** the courage to face a genuinely no-win call from principle, name its cost, and not lose yourself carrying it. The highest harvest in the whole three-year arc is not "the learner found the clever way out." It is: *the learner faced a dilemma that could not be won, made the hard call from principle, named what it cost, and did not lose themselves doing it.* S2 (sit in the unwinnable) and S7 (the third option) are reconciled here, not left at war: half the wisdom is finding the third door; the other half is knowing when there isn't one and choosing anyway, with your eyes open.

## The year's spine - three through-lines (the v0.2 elevation)

The fresh-eyes panel converged on one finding: the seven sessions are excellent competencies laid end to end, but the *year* reads as a list, not a rising action. Three complementary threads give it a spine - a destination, a carried object, and a living joy - so every session points somewhere and the whole year is one arc, not seven chapters.

1. **Name the destination on day one (Kirk).** On the first day of the year: *"In thirty weeks you will stand before your people and make a hard call in the open - the Council - and some of you will then walk out the door into your own lives - the Launch. Everything between now and then is how you get ready to be worthy of that chair."* Then reasoning (S2) is *how the captain thinks*, empathy (S4) is *how the captain feels the cost*, mentorship (S5) is *learning the terrain from those who walked it*, leadership (S6) is *how the captain holds the room*. Same content; now every session is a rung toward a mountain the learner can already see. Give them the mountain on the first day, and the whole year points.

2. **The Mantle as a physical through-line object (Bareil).** The Mantle is not taken off at the end of S1. It is *taken up* in S1 (lined with the Evidence File), *worn* into the leadership work (S6) and the Council (S7), and at the Launch the learner **decides to keep it or leave it** - the three-year arc of agency (given -> forged -> at the helm) compressed into one authored gesture. One ritual object, one gesture-language, opening in September and closing at the Launch.

3. **The Vineyard - joy that is never made useful (Guinan).** A standing weekly thread running *alongside* the seven sessions, never inside them: each learner tends something they love that has nothing to do with the curriculum (the instrument, the trail, the craft, the friendship, the game), and the cohort simply delights in it together. No Log prompt, no harvest, no developmental purpose spoken aloud. The refusal to make it useful *is* the lesson: this is what the hard calls are *for*. Without it, "love the life around it" is a slogan and the year risks teaching capable young people to steer a ship toward no port. The Vineyard lives on the fallow Friday or the tail of the lighter Tuesday - toddler-shaped, protected, glad. It is also the natural source of the *leaving-a-gift* beat at the Launch (below): the thing a learner loved here is the small sovereign gift they leave for the room.

*Why this matters (Guinan's hard truth): a resolution season built by trauma-informed voices will, by the gravity of its own care, turn every joyful thing into an instrument of safety - expression became de-roled monologue work, camaraderie became a battle-buddy watch-system. Both are good; neither is joy. The Vineyard is joy given its own home, un-conscripted.*

## The three heaviest sessions - the second-adult requirement (the load-bearing gate)

Three of the seven sessions carry material heavy enough that a solo guide cannot both hold the room's deliberation and catch the individual learner who floods. On the deep day of each, **a second adult is required in the room** - not as childcare, as a second pair of eyes whose only job is to leave with the learner who needs to leave, so the guide never has to choose between the room and the wound:

1. **S2 "No Easy Answers"** - the heaviest dilemma episodes (assisted death, coercion/torture, identity-conformity).
2. **S4 "The Stage"** - *Our Town*'s death scene (the one place the season's allegory shield does not apply, because death on the stage *is* the real thing - and a grieving learner takes a direct hit).
3. **S7 "The Council"** - public moral deliberation (exposure on exposure).

The second adult is findable informally, the way The Forge's one-morning coverage was - a trained peer, a co-op parent who has done the safety briefing, the Tots-side adult freed for that hour. Named as a **precondition, not hoped for.** Where a second adult genuinely cannot be secured for a given heavy week, the deep block for that session lightens or defers - the lighter version of the material runs, and the deep version waits until the room can be held. (This is the Guide's Own Floor working, not the guide failing.)

Partial substitute the room itself provides (K'Ehleyr, and it complements but does not replace the second adult): a taught **peer "battle-buddy" pairing** on heavy days - every learner has one other learner assigned to notice them - and a pre-established **physical exit ramp** ("here is where you step out to, and you don't have to ask"). These multiply the room's regulation capacity so the guide is not the only regulator.

### The Guide's Own Floor, amended for Year 3

The Forge amended the floor for structural (not episodic) drain - a weekly presence-check, a load-bearing witness-debrief, permission to lighten the season's shape, capacity -> enrollment -> revenue never the reverse. The Helm adds one thing, because holding assisted-death or coercion allegory *costs the holder*, and the pivot the whole safety floor depends on only works if the guide has the reserve to notice:

- **A per-heavy-session debrief** - after each of the three heaviest deep days, a named debrief with the witness-person before the next heavy session. A depleted guide cannot run the watch-and-pivot. Guide sustainability is not a nicety in Year 3; it is a safety mechanism.
- **Somatic readiness is a guide prerequisite for heavy days** (K'Ehleyr) - a dysregulated guide cannot run the Fraud Confession or *Our Town*. The nervous system reads the guide's autonomic state faster than any lesson plan can counter it.

## The safety architecture (built before the season opens)

**The somatic floor (K'Ehleyr)** - the cognitive floor (allegory, opt-out, reasoning-not-answers) is strong; the somatic floor barely existed (one de-roling ritual carrying the whole heaviest year). Build:

- **A three-scale rhythm on every heavy day:** session-open settle (extend Origin's body-first settle; binding on heavy days - regulate the room *before* activating it) -> mid-work discharge points (30-second orienting between activations, not only at the end) -> session-close return (an active downshift: orient to now, name the hard thing is over, land back in the present).
- **De-roling after every embodiment, with a real mechanic** - not once per season. Say your own name, name three things true about your real life right now ("I am [name], I am 17, it's Tuesday, I have soccer after this"), physically remove the role/prop, shake out the hands, feel the floor.
- **The environment as co-regulator** (the solo-guide substitute) - the pre-set physical exit ramp and the taught peer battle-buddy pairing on every heavy day; titrated group exposure (pairs -> small group -> full circle, paced to the room's daily window, never pushing a dysregulated room to full-circle disclosure).
- **A pre-brief before every flagged-heavy session** - name the terrain, name the exit, name that no reason is owed ("Today's story touches death and the choice to end suffering. You can step out any time, you don't owe anyone a reason, and there's water and a chair by the window"). Arrival, not ambush. The single highest-leverage, lowest-cost addition.

**The referral pathway must be LIVE before Session 1** (Bashir) - the coach-not-therapist pathway (built for Year 1) carries, but its one blank must be filled: the **named warm-referral therapist**, arranged, with the quiet space designated and the Day-1 confidentiality script ready. The Helm surfaces clinical material heavier and earlier than any prior year (mortality and assisted-death work reliably surface real grief in a small adolescent cohort - as statistics, not failure). A trauma-informed floor with an unbuilt trap door is not trauma-informed.

**The real-learner walk (Bashir + Jake), joint Salus + Jake** - the heaviest sessions are walked with a real 17-year-old before they run: the Fraud Confession rebuild, the *Our Town* death scene, the de-roling ritual. My eyes (Jake) for developmental landing, Salus's for the refusability/trauma floor. Jake's standing note, recorded: for material this heavy, he asks the walk not be captain-overridden the way the Discovery mountain gate was.

### The Fraud Confession, rebuilt (S1) - the one CONTRAINDICATED-as-written item

Bashir and Jake landed here independently and identically: **the word "everyone" is the problem.** "Everyone shares" and "opt-out available" resolve the wrong way in a real room - opting out of a shame ritual, in front of peers, at the age of peak peer-sensitivity, becomes its own shame event. The escape hatch opens onto a second trap. This is a facilitation-structure risk, not a content risk - so the fix is a facilitation rebuild, **not** a HOLD-FOR-HUMAN and **not** co-facilitation:

- **Rename it** (Jake: "the Imposter's Lie" / "Everyone's First Day") - "confession" primes shame and summons a penitent; point the name at the universal feeling, not a personal fraud.
- **The dissolving power comes from adults modeling, learners receiving.** The guide and an invited mentor share, genuinely and specifically. Learners *receive* - that alone accomplishes the goal ("accomplished people feel this too"). No circle-round, no going-around.
- **If a learner shares, it is non-attributed** - written anonymously on cards, read aloud by the guide with no author named ("someone here once felt..."). The wound of shame is in *being seen* as the fraud; anonymity keeps the universality and removes the exposure.
- **Abstention is invisible, not merely permitted** - in a "receive, then optionally write a card" structure, no one can see who chose not to write. That is the only way opt-out is real in a peer room.
- **The Evidence File is sequenced immediately after, same session** - never leave a room having just surfaced shame without building the counterweight (real evidence of competence). Stabilization inside the session, not only across it.
- **S5 mentor-rejection preload** (Jake) - reaching out to an admired professional and being ignored is a real wound at this age; teach "the non-response is not a verdict on you" *before* the outreach, not as consolation after.

### The Reasoning Standard - S2's spine (Spock)

"The win is the reasoning, not the answer" is true and *operationally empty* until someone can say what distinguishes strong reasoning from weak without smuggling in a preferred verdict. The method needs guarding at its two failure joints: collapse into relativism ("all positions equally valid") and covert indoctrination (a house answer arrived at as if discovered). The guard is a one-page instrument - the **Reasoning Standard** - five structural questions the *reasoning* must answer, none of which is "what is the right answer":

1. **Principles named.** Which two (or more) genuine goods are in conflict here? *(A "dilemma" with only one real principle is not a dilemma - it may be a rationalized atrocity; say which.)*
2. **Cost owned.** What does your chosen path cost, and to whom? *(Reasoning that claims a free path is hiding the cost.)*
3. **Steelman survived.** Can you state the strongest version of the position you rejected, well enough that its holder would say "yes, that's it"?
4. **Reversibility.** Would you accept this reasoning if you were the stakeholder it costs? *(Universalizability, in the room's grammar - the single most powerful discriminator a 17-year-old has.)*
5. **Framework visible.** Which lens are you reasoning through - consequences, duty, character, care - and what does a *different* lens see that yours does not?

The Standard disciplines the guide as much as the learner: a guide who can only steelman one side has drifted the room toward catechism. Two more S2 rules from Spock: **Kohlberg is a map of available moves, never a ladder that ranks a learner** (stages describe *arguments*, not people; name Kohlberg's own contested limits in the room - the Level-6 move applied to Level 6 itself); and **teach the distinction between a genuine dilemma and a rationalized atrocity** - Dred Scott is the study of how a system reasons itself into evil, not "the other side of a hard call." A curriculum that cannot tell these apart teaches false equivalence; one that teaches learners to tell them apart teaches the season's sharpest blade. Real cases get real-fidelity study (primary voices, the human cost named), or the pairing drops to allegory-only - honor the atrocity or do not borrow its weight.

### The Council as a real deliberative form - S7's spine (Curzon)

"They deliberate publicly" is a hope, not a container; without a form, teenagers in front of an audience default to the thing school rehearsed - debate to win. The Council is built as a real form with five parts:

- **Named, rotating roles:** a *convener* (holds the question, not a position), *voices* (argue the live positions in good faith), a *scribe* (writes the reasoning where the room can see it), and a **steward of the absent** (whose only job is "who is affected by this who is not in the room?" - the decision measured at its margins).
- **A six-phase sequence, not a melee:** Frame -> Divergence (surface every position and the fear under it, before anyone argues) -> **Steelman** (each voice states the opposing case well enough that its holder recognizes it) -> the Cost Ledger (name every path's cost and who pays, on the board) -> the Turn (real binary or false one? - the Third Chair) -> the Sense of the Room (where the reasoning arrived, and where it honestly did not).
- **The Steelman made mandatory and physical** - you may not argue your own position until you have voiced the best version of the one you reject. This is how honoring dissent gets built into the bones instead of stapled to the closing remarks. (Forum Theatre from S4 pays off here.)
- **The Third Chair (the false-binary move, made teachable):** an empty chair placed between hardened sides; four questions - what is each side protecting? is the "or" real or inherited? what would honor *both* values at once? and, when there is no third option, choose from principle and carry the grief honestly.
- **A scribe and a board** - the one hook everything hangs on. Reasoning written where all can see becomes the object in the room, not the performers; the exhibition genuinely *is* the thinking. **The strongest unresolved dissent is read into the record in the dissenter's own words and left standing, unrefuted, as part of the harvest** - a good decision carries its dissent forward visibly.
- **Consecrate and release (Bareil):** a marked opening that says "this is not ordinary talk," and - hardest and most important - a **closing that completes the council without resolving the dilemma** ("we did not solve it; we reasoned honestly, together, in front of our people; the council is closed"). That release lets a young person carry an unresolved hard thing without it becoming an open wound.

**Real stakes without a verdict (Kirk):** let the Council deliberate something with actual consequence to Vibrant Life or Boise - a real policy, a real trade-off the community faces - so the reasoning matters because it is *used*, not because it is *graded*. And S6 must add **rotating *facilitation*, not just command** (Curzon) - every learner takes the convener's chair for a low-stakes real disagreement and practices holding the question without answering it. Command teaches you to sit in the chair; facilitation teaches you to hold the room from beside it, and S7 needs the second.

## External adults - the two-track design (S5 + guest speakers)

Season Three opens the closed system to the world's adults - which is exactly right for a 17-year-old about to launch. It opens two holes in the perimeter, and they are not the same size. **A guest speaker is a window; a 1:1 mentor is a door** (Riker). Secure them differently.

### Track 1 - Boise guest speakers (the window): BUILD, solo-runnable with a modest policy

- **Guide present for the entire visit** (this alone satisfies the never-alone rule); public/observable setting; a short expectations sheet the speaker acknowledges (topic scope, no private contact exchanged with learners, no photos without consent); parent notification of who and when. **No background check required** for a one-time supervised group speaker - the supervision *is* the control, and requiring checks would kill the ability to bring interesting people in.
- **Watch the conversion point:** a speaker who starts contacting a learner afterward has walked through the door without being vetted for it - route any follow-up through the guide, which converts them to the mentor track.

### Track 2 - 1:1 professional mentors (the door): BUILD-WITH-CONDITIONS, not cleared to launch until the infrastructure exists

Riker's gates (all binding, mentor track): **observable channel only** (no private DMs / personal social media / off-platform; the guide sees the existence and cadence of contact, never the content for assessment); **never-alone satisfied structurally** (default remote-and-monitored; in-person only with Erin present or a public setting - never a private residence or vehicle); **background check** via an external screening process before first contact; **three-party informed consent** (parent, learner, mentor) + **signed boundaries**; a **no-fault off-ramp** (a learner ends or reports a match with one word to Erin, no reason owed, no confrontation); a **written safeguarding + mandatory-reporting policy** authored once, on paper, before the first match; and **counsel review** of Idaho duty-of-care, mandatory reporting, and liability/insurance (same routing as the teen-sandbox §7 crux).

Kasidy's reciprocity floor (so we honor givers, not mine them for "inspiration"): **pay real honoraria** + cover costs (a waived fee is accepted only if freely offered, never solicited); offer a **menu of depths** - one informational interview / a short arc / a sustained 1:1 - with **the one-off honored identically to the year-long mentorship** (no ranking the givers; refusal #5 turned toward the adults); **bounded terms + a dignified no-fault exit**; **the teen arrives prepared** (the "art of being mentored" as a precondition - a good question is the reciprocal gift); **close the loop** (genuine thanks, tell the giver what their gift did, ask whether it served *them*); **the story is a gift, never the price** (a professional's hard call - especially a trauma-proximate one - is invited freely, never required as content); **continuity over churn** (build the network to compound toward the givers across years - eventually, learners who were mentored returning to mentor); and **the mentor is not the load-bearing safeguarding adult** (keep that boundary bright; route weight it wasn't built for to the guide).

**Ordering (non-negotiable):** Riker builds the container first; then Kasidy's reciprocity design; George costs the honoraria and does the reciprocal outreach to Boise professional communities (build through communities that already hold relationship, not cold extraction); Kira/Stirps if the network touches refugee or immigrant communities (the Closing Feast cultural-respect gate travels with it).

### The Launch built as a true rite of passage (Bareil + Guinan + Ezri)

The fresh-eyes panel's hardest finding: the most weight in the whole three-year design rests on its thinnest container. The Launch - the single most important threshold in nine years - was one line. Build it as a real rite of passage, with the three movements every enduring threshold-crossing has, or it will feel like a graduation (a ceremony that happens *to* you, not one you *cross through*):

1. **Separation - the un-belonging.** The part everyone skips because it hurts, and skipping it is why so many graduations feel hollow and so many young adults feel they never really left. A moment that names the leaving directly: *this was your home; you are being released from it.* The **grief-and-gratitude beat lives here** as the *work* of this phase, not decoration - grief is the honest cost of having belonged to something worth grieving. *Caution (Guinan):* do **not** borrow the de-roling mechanic for this grief. De-roling steps you out of a role that was never you; leaving home is stepping out of a self that absolutely *was* you. Never teach a young person to de-role their own real sorrow. Let it be tender, held, unrushed, with a real exit (coach-not-therapist holds; a learner leaving an unstable home for an uncertain future is not only excited).

2. **Liminality - the threshold, held.** The between-space, no-longer-a-learner and not-yet-launched. The **Star Chart is revealed here as a threshold-object, not a graduation slide** - the map of where they have been, given at the exact moment they must navigate by it alone, with a silence around it that no one fills. The between-space is where the soul does its work.

3. **Reincorporation - the crossing, witnessed, with a change of standing.** The actual step into adulthood, seen by their people, across a literal threshold (a doorway, a line stepped over), Log in hand, into a circle of adults who **receive them as an adult and address them differently on the far side.** A rite that does not change your standing in your community's eyes is a going-away party. The **Captain's Log handoff** is the return-object and the reincorporation gesture ("it is yours; take it; no copy kept"). And the learner **decides what to do with the Mantle** - keep it (this is who I am now) or leave it (it belonged to this place) - either choice true, the *choosing* being the rite, the three-year arc of agency in one gesture.

**Leaving a gift (Guinan).** The rite is built around what the learner *takes*; a threshold is also a community losing a member. The launched learner **leaves one small, sovereign thing behind for the room** - a word to the next captain, a mark, their Vineyard-thing - and the **cohort marks the departure *as a cohort*** (not the guide performing it at them). The threshold felt from both sides of the door. For the founding cohort - the first ever to complete the cycle - what they leave becomes the room's first inheritance, turning a series of departures into a lineage.

**The family-less learner is most exposed here** - "crossing witnessed by your people" is the day the young person with no people falls hardest. Name the tribe as *your people*, in advance and explicitly - the same inclusion floor the Unveiling and Closing Feast hold, applied to the most tender day of nine years.

**The Launch rite, sequenced** (structure now, copy later, with the cohort): guide opens the rite (never the system) -> **SEPARATION:** look back and read the Growth Record together (arrive at where you've been) + the grief-and-gratitude beat (the release, held, unrushed) -> **LIMINALITY:** the Dome opens, the Star Chart given as a threshold-object in silence -> craft the next-step document -> **REINCORPORATION:** the handoff (generate the Portrait export, choose its visibility, take it - this beat cannot be ceremony over a placeholder) + the Mantle keep-or-leave choice + leaving a gift for the room + the witnessed crossing into a circle that receives them as an adult -> the Launch (the door closes and stays unlocked from her side; no come-back nudge).

## Ring-1 applied fixes (2026-08-28) - The Helm's share

The three-year spec carries the canonical Ring-1 doctrine (its "Ring-1 revisions" section); the Helm's concrete applications - the heaviest year, so the safety fixes concentrate here:

- **Scaffolding vs floor - titrate the signal, hold the floor (SSC ruling).** The Helm's stacked container was found to over-*signal* ("we expect you to break"). Titrate the *reassurance signal* - reframe every net as legible trust, not vigilance (the battle-buddy is "a crew that holds each other," never "assigned to watch you flood"); thin the reassurance as the cohort's trust matures. But **hold the floor's actual availability whole** - the exit ramp, discharge/return, de-roling after every embodiment, the second adult on the three heaviest sessions are non-negotiable and are named un-cuttable *in the same breath as the referral pathway* (a tired guide cuts de-roling first because it feels like overhead - it is the floor).
- **The harden-off, RESEATED (SSC/COC ruling; the PDC's proposal refused as posed).** A learner meeting a hard thing *un-held* before launch is a real and good goal - but grief and moral injury do not "harden off" (that is the frost, not the bracing morning). So it is **refused on the heaviest material (S2/S4/S7), where the floor stays whole**, and reseated onto ordinary-stakes surfaces that are *meant* to be crossed alone: the absent-guide day, the real bureaucracy call, mentor rejection, the no-win case. There, reduce the *visible scaffold* (not the floor's availability), on *medium-weight* material, *chosen and named* never sprung, age/tenure gated (launching 17-18s in their final season only; contraindicated for under-16 / first-season / already-over-hardened learners - Kira).
- **The closing practice reaches its culmination here.** The learner-held closing/re-crossing practice, seeded in Origin and spiraled, is what the graduate carries out the door to close their own openings after the guide is gone; the Launch rite's Separation -> Liminality -> Reincorporation is the arc-scale instance of it. The design opens faster than it closes - this is the year the closing capacity must be *theirs*.
