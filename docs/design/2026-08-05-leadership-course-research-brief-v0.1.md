## The question

What actually builds a young person into someone others *choose* to follow - and what does the
leadership-industrial complex sell instead? Separate the books leaders privately credit for how they
lead from the books sold *as* leadership.

## The anchor (the thread to pull)

The fork the fleet already names: **Greenleaf's *Servant Leadership* (1970)** vs **Greene's *48 Laws
of Power* (1998)**. One says leadership is service you're worth following for; the other is a manual
for power *over* people. Every leadership book sits somewhere on that line - find where each actually
sits, not where it's marketed.

## The lens

*All of it - find the truth.* Before-they-led admissions outrank stage/podcast recommendations, the
way before-they-were-rich admissions (Buffett reading Graham at 19) outranked Davos signaling.

## Structural hypotheses to test

1. **Credited != sold.** The books leaders quietly credit and the books that sell *as* "leadership"
   are two different lists - probably inverted (reader-impact vs author-profit).
2. **Old canon = character; new canon = influence.** Pre-internet leadership = *who you are*
   (character, service, responsibility); post-2010 drifts to *how you're perceived* (personal brand,
   influence, "executive presence"). Name the drift.
3. **The extraction line.** Leadership-as-service (fill a real need for a group; be worth following)
   forks into leadership-as-domination (manufacture followers, power for its own sake - the
   Dale-Carnegie-through-Greene lineage of *managing* people). The Karbo-equivalent here is the "how
   to make people do what you want" book.

## The Evoked angle - the firewall (the essay only we'd write)

The one honest test: *does this book build the capacity to serve a group, or the feeling of power
over people?* The tell is who it centers - **does it teach you to be worth following, or to make
others follow?** A leadership book whose core promise is *"and they'll do what you want"* sells the
same wish Karbo sold: dominance without the work of deserving trust.

## Adversarial discipline (the skeptic node with kill authority)

- **Promise made / promise kept, per book** (Ishka's lens); *kill* weak attributions the way the
  money report killed the Hill/Carnegie fabrication. Watch for leaders crediting the prestige book,
  not the load-bearing one.
- **Primary reads, honestly sourced** - deep-read the real arguments (Greenleaf's actual essay, not a
  summary). **Study Greene as a specimen, never as a source** (the standing "clean primary, NOT
  Greene" firewall - read the ad, skip the pill).
- Skeptic gets a disconfirmation mandate + cold re-instancing each loop; **zero kills in round one =
  a broken skeptic, halt and inspect** (per the 2026-08-04 research-loop discipline).

## Child-facing / developmental constraints

- **Capacity, not a title.** "Leader" != being in charge; never "how to get people to follow you." At
  ~8, leadership = *going first, helping a group*; at ~15, organizing + carrying responsibility. No
  "influence / personal brand / executive presence" language for children.
- **Beneficence is native, not bolted on:** leadership *is* service - that's the honest definition
  and the firewall in one move.

## Output shape (sized to feed the course)

A tiered canon (Character / Engine / World, like the money tiers) + 2-3 primary reads + the firewall
test, structured to drop into a **3-stage / 4-step** Leadership course - and to validate or replace
the placeholder arc stubbed in code (`js/pillars/life-skills.js` COURSES.leadership: **Serve →
Steady → Gather**), each step universal and same-day-doable.

## Graph structure (fan-out → skeptic → synthesize; loop if iterating)

- **Synthesizer:** the tiered report + the firewall + the course-ready arc.
