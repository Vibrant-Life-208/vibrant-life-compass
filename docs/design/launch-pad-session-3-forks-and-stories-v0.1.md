# The Launch Pad - Session 3 companion: the forks, the stories, the grid

**The three things Session 3 asked for and did not have: a fork bank with ceilings, a named story ladder, and a morning a guide can follow holding a baby**
**Status:** v0.1 - authored 2026-10-21 from the eight-voice Session 3 room (Ludus, Hoshi, Salus, Jake, Polaris, Troi, Ezri, Fabula)
**Canonical parent:** `docs/lesson-plans/origin-lesson-plan-book.md`, Part 3, Session 3. The book carries the flow; this page carries the detail.
**Companions:** the movement floor (the settle, the way down, the exit ramp); the closing practice; the coach-not-therapist referral pathway.

> **Why this page exists.** Session 3 is three weeks long, its opening ritual is daily, and two of its six guided mornings are federal holidays. It was the one session whose "what you'll need" list named a category instead of an artifact: *safe-distance stories or clips.* A blank in a needs list is the place a Tuesday improvises, and this is the session where improvising costs the most. Three things are written down here so they are not invented at 9:05 with a worn infant on the front: the fork, the story, and the shape of the morning.

---

## Part 1 - The fork (the fixed daily opener)

### The form, which does not change

Every guided morning of Session 3 opens the same way, after the settle.

1. **The guide poses one small real choice.** Real means it has an actual consequence in the next hour or the next day. Small means the consequence is small.
2. **Everybody chooses.** Out loud, by pointing, by writing it on the card, by standing on one side of a line. Any of those count equally and all of them are on the table before anyone needs one.
3. **The room traces it.** Two questions, always these two: *what did that open?* and *what did it quietly close?* The second question is the whole teaching. There is no free move.
4. **It ends.** No winner is named, nothing is recorded, nobody is asked to defend a choice.

About three minutes. Then the way down if it ran hot, then the day's work.

**The sameness is the medicine.** This is the same reasoning the movement floor runs on: one vocabulary learned well enough that a flooded seventeen-year-old drops into it without thinking. Five different exercises across five mornings teaches a room to expect novelty. One form, five mornings, teaches a body that choosing on purpose is a thing it does. The session is named for a muscle. A muscle is built by reps of the same movement.

### The five rules

These are floor, not furniture. They are what keeps a daily ritual from becoming a daily test.

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| **A ceiling.** About three minutes, no winner, no score, nothing kept, never twice in a morning. Ends early if it runs hot. | Unbounded play becomes performance becomes habit becomes something else. Every play surface in this programme declares its ceiling. |
| **No consequence the guide hands out.** The consequence traced is the one the chooser names. | A fork whose outcome the adult dispenses teaches that the adult holds the outcome. That is the inverse of the session. |
| **A non-speaking way in, built rather than permitted.** Pointed, written, or placed. | A fork that needs a quick voice is a fork somebody sits out of, and in a daily ritual that somebody is seen sitting out five times. Permission beside the ritual is not a door; it is a note about a door. |
| **No tradeoff that assumes resources.** | Options that cost money, travel, or a free Saturday are not available to every learner in the room. Keep the stakes inside what everyone actually holds. |
| **Nobody improvises in front of the room.** | A learner who freezes in a scene has been shown publicly that they cannot choose, in the session about choosing. Freeze is not a choice. Performance forms are volunteer-only or absent. |

### The bank (the optional second slot, some days, never the opener)

Use at most one of these in a morning, after the fork, when the day has room. Each one carries its own note.

- **Doors.** Name a choice you already made this week. What opened. What closed. No judgment on either. *Runs quiet; good on a heavy morning; works entirely in writing.*
- **The default.** Name one thing you do without deciding. Try deciding it on purpose for one day and report nothing. *The week 11 form. Guide goes first, with a real one, badly.*
- **One small path.** A branching story read aloud where an early small choice cascades. The room votes at each branch. *Light taste of consequence, not the Year 3 work. Nobody speaks alone.*
- **Would you rather, inside the room.** Two options that both genuinely cost something, both of them things every learner in the room actually holds: your Tuesday morning block, where you sit, who you partner with, which thing you finish first. *Rule 4 is doing the work here. If an option needs a car or a credit card, it is the wrong option.*
- **The reasoned no.** A learner declines something small, and the room hears the reasoning. *This is the session's thesis practiced on the studio itself. Voluntary, always; it is the most exposed form in the bank and belongs late in the session, not early.*

**Retired from the earlier bank:** yes/no improv. A scene in which players may only say "yes-and" or "no" is a performance form with a freeze risk in the one session that must not teach a learner that they cannot choose. The sensation it was reaching for (feel how each shapes what is possible) is carried by the fork's second question, with no audience.

## Part 2 - The stories (three rungs, graded by weight)

**The rule: one per guided morning, never two. Never the top rung on the first morning. Safe distance means not their life; it does not mean weightless, and the weight climbs.**

Every story answers one question: *where did one choice change everything?* And every story gets the same second question, which is the honest one: *and what did it close?*

### Rung one - ordinary and small

Where the ladder starts. The point is that the mechanism is visible in a life the size of theirs.

- **A choice from the guide's own week.** Yours, small, told plainly, including what it closed. You go first on this ladder the same way you go first on defaults.
- **A choice from the studio's own history.** Something the tribe decided in Session 1 or 2 and has been living inside since. The agreements are the obvious one.
- **A fork in a book or film the room already shares.** Learner-supplied is better than guide-supplied here. Ask the night before.

### Rung two - real, costly, and someone else's

Mid-weight. A real person, a real cost, at genuine distance.

- **Claudette Colvin, Montgomery, 2 March 1955.** Fifteen years old, nine months before Rosa Parks, refused to move from her seat and was arrested. The movement later chose a different test case. **Why it is the strongest rung-two story in this room:** the chooser is the age of the youngest learner in it, the choice was real, it cost her, and it did not change everything in the way the usual version of these stories promises. That is a truer teaching than triumph. **Watch-for, and it is firm:** the reasons the NAACP passed over her are discussed in the record and include her age and her pregnancy. Do not make a pregnant teenager a lesson in a room of teenagers. If the room asks, the honest answer is that adults decided she was not the right symbol, and that is its own hard thing about choice.
- **"The Road Not Taken," Robert Frost, 1916.** Read the whole poem, not the last three lines. The two roads were worn "really about the same." The speaker says he *will be* telling it with a sigh, "ages and ages hence," as though one made all the difference. **The honest reading is the one this session wants:** the difference was authored afterward, by the teller. **Taught the usual way it is the wrong story for this session** - it would say your choices make you singular - and taught correctly it is close to the best one, because it is about the story we tell about our own choosing. If the room has only ever met the greeting-card version, saying so is part of the work.

### Rung three - heavy, and only if the room has earned it

Late in the session, on a morning with room, with the exit ramp named first.

- **Stanislav Petrov, 26 September 1983.** A Soviet early-warning officer saw his satellite system report American missile launches, judged it a false alarm, and did not pass it up the chain. It was a false alarm. One man, one night, one judgment, against his own instruments. **Why it works at rung three:** the choice is unmistakable, the stakes are total, and it is at maximum distance from a sixteen-year-old's life. **Watch-for:** it is a nuclear-annihilation story. Preview it, name what it touches, name the exit, and do not run it the same morning as anything else heavy.
- **A learner-supplied heavy one.** If a learner brings one, it is theirs and it is better than ours. Ask what they want done with it before the room gets it.

**What is deliberately not on this ladder.** Nothing where the chooser is harmed by their own choice in a way the room will read as deserved. Nothing where the lesson lands as "she should have chosen differently." The session's load-bearing safety line is that nobody chose their harm; a story that implies otherwise undoes three weeks of careful framing in one morning.

## Part 3 - The grid (the morning a guide can follow)

**Written to the five-guide-day shape**, which is the shape if Election Day is kept as a civics morning and Veterans Day is observed. **The holiday call is the captain's and it changes the denominator**, so the four-day fallback is named below the table. Read the call first, then the grid.

| | Guided morning | Opener | The work | Story | Walk-in question | Close |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Tue Nov 2 (Election Day, kept as civics) | Fork, taught explicitly as the session's fixed ritual | Re-thread first, short. Then the knot: responsibility, freedom, choice as one thing. Then the honest frame, both halves, including room. | None. The first morning carries the frame, not a story. | How was the break | Settle close, three prompts |
| **2** | Thu Nov 4 | Fork | "Where did one choice change everything?" at safe distance, then the trace. Optional second slot: Doors. | **Rung one.** Guide's own week. | Anything louder at home | Way down if hot, then three prompts |
| **3** | Tue Nov 9 | Fork | Doors that open and close: no free move. Then defaults and drift. **Guide names one of her own defaults first, badly.** If this is week 11's only morning, keep defaults and let doors go. | **Rung two.** Colvin or Frost, not both. | Anything louder at home, each learner, one at a time | Three prompts |
| **4** | Tue Nov 16 | Fork | Each learner names a real choice in front of them, or one they are still circling, and practices deciding it out loud in their own frame. Not "what should I do" handed to the guide. | **Rung three**, only if the room has room for it. Otherwise none. | Anything louder at home | Way down, then three prompts |
| **5** | Thu Nov 18 | Fork, the last one, named as the last one | The harvest: a choice I own, or none owned yet. The shift from "things happen to me" to "things happen, and then I choose." Then the pace question. Then the bridge to Running My Own Ship. | None. The harvest is the room's own story. | Asked before the circle, not in it | **Closing minute at session scale.** Then the three sentences said out loud: the one adult, 988, and that you are not reachable. |

**Own-days (Mon and Wed) do not change shape this session.** Block one is core and the credit plan; block two is the project and the writing; the midday check-in happens. The one Session 3 addition: if a learner's choice-work has opened friction at home, the check-in is where that gets heard, not the circle.

**The four-day fallback** (both holidays observed): cut morning 4. Move the real-choice work onto the back half of morning 3 and give morning 5 its full harvest. Do not compress the harvest and do not drop the send-off. The send-off is the single most load-bearing beat in the session, because this session ends into the holiday where the work meets the family it is about.

**The six-day shape** (neither holiday observed): morning 4 becomes Thu Nov 11, the real-choice work gets its own morning, and morning 5 at Nov 16 gains a second story slot at rung two. The grid above is the floor, not the ceiling.

## What this page does not do

- It does not discharge the **consented real-learner walk** that Salus and Jake hold. Nothing here has been met by a sixteen-year-old. A fork bank with ceilings is a better artifact than a blank; it is not a watched room.
- It does not fill the **named-therapist line** in the referral pathway, which is still the one line in that document that must not stay blank before the year opens.
- It does not resolve the **fifteen-to-eighteen span in one room**, open since the three-year spec. Rule 4 and the circling clause reduce the exposure; they do not close the question.

*"We evoke - we never extract." - a session about choosing has to be a place where the learner with the least room still gets a real rep.*
