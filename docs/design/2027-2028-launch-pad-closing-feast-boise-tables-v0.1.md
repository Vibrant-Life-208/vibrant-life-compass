# The Closing Feast - Boise's Tables

**A recurring Launch Pad ritual: end each session by experiencing a different food-world, with the Boise owners who make it**
**Status:** v0.1 working draft - research-seeded; partners are LEADS to verify, not confirmed
**Companions:** studio-year-plan v0.3; the three-year spec's "Live & Love Life" thread ("a feast at every harvest")

## What it is

Each session (every 3-6 weeks) closes with a shared gathering meal that lets the tribe experience a food-world new to them - and, wherever possible, meet the **owner** behind it: a real person, often with a real journey. Not catering. Food *and* story *and* a real relationship, done as a reciprocal expedition into Boise's own tables.

It is the spec's "expeditions" practice + The Table's belonging + the interviewing through-skill (family → stakeholders → mentors → culture-and-story-bearers), all in one ritual. A restaurant owner who came to Boise as a refugee, telling a room of teenagers their own story over their own food, is the *Midnight Gospel* "an ordinary life carries enormous meaning" lesson made edible.

## The arc across the year (the world-table progression)

- **Session 2 (The Lineage)** already has the family-story potluck - foods of *where you come from*.
- **The other sessions' Closing Feasts go outward** - a food-world *beyond* your own.
- So across the year the tribe travels from "where I come from" to "the whole wide table" - mirroring the Origin-outward arc.

## How a Closing Feast runs

- **Learner-voiced:** each session, a learner or small group picks the food-world and helps host - why it drew them, what to know before you taste. It becomes a small quest (research + reach-out + present), exercising the interviewing skill.
- **Two formats:** (a) an **expedition** - the tribe visits the restaurant, meets the owner, hears their story, shares the meal on-site; or (b) the **owner comes to the studio** for a meal + their story. Alternate by session and by what each owner prefers.
- **The owner tells their own story, first-person.** Sovereignty: never the guide narrating "their exotic culture." The teens ask, listen, and learn the person, not just the plate.

## Reciprocity - the non-negotiable (evoke, never extract)

A small or immigrant-owned restaurant is not a free cultural exhibit. Every Closing Feast must leave the owner **better off**:
- **Pay real custom** - a paid meal / a paid visiting-host fee. Never "come talk to our kids for free."
- **Honor their time and expertise** as you would a mentor's.
- **Their story is theirs** - told first-person, shared only as they wish, never recorded or published without explicit consent.
- **Mutual benefit** - income, their work valued by young people, their story genuinely heard. If the exchange only serves the studio, it is extraction wearing a field-trip's clothes - do not run it.
- **Work through the community infrastructure** where you can (resettlement agencies below) so outreach is warm and culturally brokered, not cold.

## The inclusion floor (carried from The Table)

- Base food always provided; no learner ever pays-to-participate or is exposed for not bringing/affording.
- Allergen / dietary / religious safety planned in advance - real food this learner can gladly eat.
- **Presence, not consumption** - "taste what you like"; no one's plate is watched (ED-safe). A "try everything" feast must have a graceful opt-out.
- Cultural respect (Kira/Stirps lens): honor the cuisine and its people; "bring your table to our table," every family's food is real food - never exoticize.
