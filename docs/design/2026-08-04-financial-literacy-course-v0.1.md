## The spine (binding - from the 2026-07-20 money-ethics ruling)

- Aim is a **capacity, not a number**: *free with, capable with, generous with money.* "Millionaire" /
  "be rich" appears nowhere - it is the Klontz money-worship script (Klontz & Britt 2011), empirically
  tied to *worse* financial health; materialistic aspirations predict *lower* wellbeing (Kasser).
- **Beneficence is the keystone** (Frontiers 2018 "four pathways") - earn-to-give-back is the bridge
  that makes money-work good for the person, not just the balance. Maps to Europa's own value.
- **Experiential, not lectured** (Whitebread & Bingham 2013; CFPB Building Blocks) - direct instruction
  fails; kids learn money by doing it small and real. Every step below is a doing.
- **Overjustification caveat** - never wire the course to pay a child for a passion they already love.

## The arc (from the books - the honest, restraint-first canon)

Babylon (keep) -> Wardlaw (earn by serving) -> Graham + Munger (grow safely). Evoked's own thread,
the **extraction firewall**, runs through all three: honest money is hard, slow, mostly what you do
NOT do; the wish-sale is easy, fast, belief. The one test: **does this leave someone with a capacity
they did not have, or only a feeling?** The tell is always the promise about effort.

## Stage 1 - KEEP  ·  *"A part of all you earn is yours to keep."* (Clason, 1926)

The foundation. Needs no capital, no talent, no permission. Boring on purpose - which is why it is
the most honest book in the canon (soil, not seed).

1. **The tenth.** Set aside a part of any money you have now, however small (Arkad's tenth) - a real
   jar or account. The act, not the amount, is the lesson.
2. **Need vs want, one week.** Sort what money goes toward into *need* / *want* - no judgment, just
   notice. (Babylon: "control thy expenditures.")
3. **Find one leak.** Spot one thing money quietly slips out on that you do not actually value;
   redirect it into the tenth.
4. **Name your "free with."** What would having-enough-set-aside let you *not worry about*? (Capacity,
   not a target.)

## Stage 2 - EARN  ·  *"Find a need and fill it."* (Wardlaw, 1958) + the firewall

Money comes from serving a real need. This is also where the con gets learned - "find a need and fill
it" forks into "manufacture a need and sell relief from the anxiety you just amplified."

1. **Find a real need.** Spot one thing someone around you actually needs done or made (real, not
   invented).
2. **Fill it once, for real.** Do it. Notice the thing itself: *earning is serving.*
3. **The honest test (firewall).** Learn the one question and use it on a real ad/offer: *does this
   sell a capacity, or just a feeling?* The tell is the promise about effort - "you won't have to try"
   is the signature of the wish-sale (Hill, Karbo).
4. **Give one back.** Set aside a part of what you earned for someone or something beyond you.
   (Beneficence - the keystone.)

## Stage 3 - GROW & GUARD  ·  Graham (1949) + Munger + Babylon's Laws

Make it work without gambling. Every rule here is in Babylon's Laws of Gold *and* Graham - the canon
agrees with itself across a century.

1. **Make a coin work.** Put a small saved amount somewhere it grows (age-real example). "Every gold
   piece you save is a slave to work for you."
2. **Circle of competence.** Only put money into what you *understand* - Babylon's 4th Law ("gold
   slippeth away from the man who invests in businesses with which he is not familiar") = Graham's
   thorough-analysis rule, verbatim, 23 years earlier. Practice telling "I get this" from "I don't."
3. **Margin of safety.** Never risk what you cannot get back; principal safe first (Graham's border
   between investing and gambling).
4. **Spot the trickster + go slow on purpose.** Babylon's 5th Law: "gold flees the man who would force
   it to impossible earnings or followeth the alluring advice of tricksters and schemers." Find one
   real "fast / easy / guaranteed" pitch and name the tell. Then the payoff: real growth = time +
   restraint, boring on purpose - the truest money book is the least sellable one.
