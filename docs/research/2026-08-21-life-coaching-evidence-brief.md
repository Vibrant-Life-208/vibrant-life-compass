# Life Coaching - Evidence Brief for the Launch Pad Guide Model

**Date:** 2026-08-21
**Purpose:** On-hand reference for the "guide as life-coach, not teacher" model. The guide equips learners across the three-year wheel (Origin - Forge - Helm) through coaching, not instruction. This brief records what the evidence says about that model, its risks, and what to carry into the guide spec.
**Method:** WebSearch, Aug 2026. Sources are secondary/summary-level; primary papers linked where available.

---

## The findings

**1. The coaching *method* has a real, moderate evidence base - for the exact outcomes Launch Pad targets.**
A meta-analysis restricted to randomized controlled trials found coaching produces a moderate overall effect (Hedges' g approximately 0.59), with significant gains in **self-efficacy, goal attainment, resilience, and psychological capital** - the capacity-building the studio is built around. Researcher caveats: significant publication bias; effects larger for *self-reported* than *observed* outcomes; effects larger with **qualified** coaches.
- https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/what-can-we-know-about-the-effectiveness-of-coaching-a-meta-analy/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10272735/ (executive-coaching RCT meta-analysis)

**2. "Life coaching" as a brand has thin evidence; the goal-focused Socratic *technique* is the evidenced part.**
A systematic review of life coaching for health outcomes found only a handful of studies (two RCTs) and could offer "only tendencies," not conclusions. Takeaway: build on the coaching *practice* (goal-focus, Socratic questioning, accountability), not on the "life coach" label.
- https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK169370/

**3. The field is completely unregulated - its defining risk.**
As of 2026: no state licenses, no mandated standards, no oversight body. Anyone can call themselves a life coach. ICF's ACC credential (60+ training hours) is the voluntary benchmark, but weekend-certificate coaches are equally legal. The field has attracted "theft, coercion, and cult-like behaviors," and de-licensed therapists sometimes rebrand as coaches to keep practicing under the radar.
- https://lifecoachingcertification.net/blog/certification-requirements/
- https://psyche.co/ideas/life-coaching-is-unregulated-and-growing-rapidly-should-it-be-reined-in
- https://www.mic.com/life/inside-the-wild-unregulated-world-of-life-coaching

**4. The coach/therapist boundary is the specific danger - and for coaches it is unenforced.**
Coaching = goals, accountability, forward momentum with a *non-clinical* population. Therapy = assessment, diagnosis, treatment of mental-health conditions, trauma, distress. Scope-of-practice violation is a license-losing offense for therapists and "essentially unregulated" for coaches.
- https://www.blueprint.ai/blog/life-coach-vs-therapist-defining-boundaries-and-collaborative-opportunities-in-clinical-practice

**5. Youth coaching: soft evidence, but the active ingredients are developmentally sound.**
Sources were mostly industry (Youth Coaching Institute and similar), not peer-reviewed - treat "evidence-based youth coaching" as marketing-adjacent. What holds up developmentally: **a one-on-one relationship with a caring adult, competency development, and time.**
- https://www.youthcoachinginstitute.com/evidence-based-coaching-for-resilience-in-teens-and-emerging-adults/

---

## What it means for Launch Pad

**The model is well-chosen.** The "guide as life-coach, not teacher" approach - Socratic, holds questions, WOOP goals, accountability without surveillance, a sustained caring-adult relationship - is the *evidenced-good* part of coaching. It maps directly onto the self-efficacy and resilience gains the research supports, and onto the developmentally-sound active ingredients of youth work. This is the part with the effect size, not the fluffy part.

**The one carry-forward risk: the therapy boundary.** The biggest failure mode in coaching is drifting into therapy - and Launch Pad points a coach at therapy-adjacent material (identity work, family lineage, mortality, the autobiography's "worst day," Year 3 shadow work and the Fraud Confession) with 15-18-year-olds. Clinical material *will* surface. Two things to carry into the guide spec:

1. **The three-year spec's safety floors already ARE the coach-not-therapist boundary in curriculum form** (allegory-first, no forced self-disclosure, opt-out without explanation, dilemmas resolve into reasoning). The research explains why they are load-bearing, not optional. This is the same wall behind Wellness's HOLD-FOR-HUMAN.
2. **Add an explicit referral pathway.** When something clinical surfaces, the guide's job is to notice, hold with care, and *route to a licensed professional* - never to treat. Name this in the guide spec.

**On legitimacy:** do not lean on "life coach" as a credential with parents - it is legally meaningless and tainted by bad actors. Legitimacy comes from the practice and the safety architecture. Naming the boundary to families ("a mentor and guide, not a therapist, with a clear path to real help when needed") is itself a trust signal in an overclaiming field.
