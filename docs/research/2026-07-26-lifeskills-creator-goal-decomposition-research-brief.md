# Life Skills vs Creator Mindset - Goal Decomposition Research Brief

**Date:** 2026-07-26
**Purpose:** Internal product-design decision for Vibrant Life Compass. Not public content.
**Scope:** Four scoped questions on goal-setting across two Compass pillars - LIFE SKILLS (capacities: leadership, entrepreneurship, financial literacy, wellness) and CREATOR MINDSET (dispositions: growth mindset, emotional regulation, curiosity, responsibility).
**Standard:** Match the fleet intellectual-honesty bar. Flag contested vs well-supported claims. A clean "we don't know" beats a confident overclaim.

---

## Executive Summary

The evidence points to a clear structural answer: **the way you decompose a goal should depend on what kind of thing it is, and Compass currently applies one shape (goal-with-a-success-line) to two categories that behave differently.**

1. **WOOP / MCII is well-supported** as a self-regulation technique, including a direct randomized trial with children (Duckworth et al. 2013) and a 2021 meta-analysis (small-to-medium effect, g ~ 0.34). But its evidence base is about *turning a concrete wish into action on identifiable obstacles* - not about "achieving" a broad year-long capacity. WOOP works best when the wish is specific and near-term. Applied to a broad capacity, WOOP is fine as a *scaffold for the next concrete step*, not as a "complete the capacity by year-end" contract.

2. **The category-error crux: YES, treating a disposition as a "goal with a success line" is a category error** - and this is one of the better-supported findings in the brief, resting on goal-setting theory's own internal logic (Seijts & Latham), not on the contested mindset literature. Performance/outcome goals demonstrably *harm* performance on complex, skill-acquisition tasks; the correct instrument for those is a **learning goal** (focus on strategies/process) or scaffolded practice, not a deadline with a success line. Dispositions (growth mindset, emotion regulation, curiosity, responsibility) are the least "achievable-by-deadline" objects of all - they are cultivated, state-by-state, over developmental time.

3. **Growth mindset specifically is the most contested claim in the entire Compass model.** The strongest recent evidence (Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023, preregistered, 122 studies) found the average effect indistinguishable from zero once study quality and publication bias are accounted for. The real-but-modest effect survives only for lower-achieving students in supportive contexts (Yeager et al. 2019). Compass's existing hedge ("not magic on its own") is correct and should be preserved - and this finding is a strong argument *against* asking a learner to set growth mindset as a "goal."

4. **Progress-without-a-score is achievable** via scaffold fading / gradual-release-of-responsibility, which is a validated *pedagogical* framework. The operationalization risk is real: any fading measure can silently become the metric it was trying to avoid. The safe design shows the *learner* their own reduced reliance narratively, privately, and without comparison.

5. **Autonomy support (SDT) is well-supported** and gives concrete design levers: meaningful choice, rationale, the learner's own language, no rewards/pressure, non-judgmental feedback. This is the sovereignty spine and it is fully compatible with the recommended split below.

**Bottom line for Compass:** Keep goal-with-success-line for LIFE SKILLS *capacities decomposed to concrete behaviors* (WOOP is a good fit here). For CREATOR MINDSET *dispositions*, replace the "goal + success line" object with a **practice / learning-goal object** (a way of engaging + noticing movement) rather than an achievement to be completed by a deadline.

## Question 1 - Life Skills Goals: Is WOOP well-supported for turning a broad capacity into an adolescent goal?

### Findings

**WOOP's evidence base is genuine and holds up (currency-checked).**

- WOOP is the plain-language name for **Mental Contrasting with Implementation Intentions (MCII)**, developed by Gabriele Oettingen. It combines mental contrasting (Wish -> Outcome -> Obstacle) with Gollwitzer's if-then implementation intentions (Plan). ([Oettingen / Frontiers overview](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.565202/full))
- **Meta-analysis (2021):** 21 studies, 24 effect sizes, 15,907 participants. MCII produced a **small-to-medium effect on goal attainment (g = 0.336)**, with stronger effects for in-person delivery (g = 0.465) than document-based delivery (g = 0.277). ([PMC8149892](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8149892/)) - Note the delivery moderator: an app-based (document-like) WOOP should expect the lower end of the range.
- **Direct child evidence:** Duckworth, Kirby, Gollwitzer & Oettingen (2013), "From Fantasy to Action," randomized 77 urban 5th-graders to MCII vs a positive-thinking control. MCII significantly improved report-card grades, attendance, and conduct. ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/256294319); [Duckworth PDF](https://uploads-ssl.webflow.com/59faaf5b01b9500001e95457/5bc55b08a8a9d854aace2c9f_Duckworth,%20A.%20L.,%20Kirby,%20T.%20A.,%20Gollwitzer,%20A.,%20&%20Oettingen,%20G.%202013.pdf))
- Implementation intentions have independent RCT support in adolescents for concrete behaviors (e.g., smoking-initiation prevention). ([PMC6474716](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6474716/))

**The important caveat - broad capacity vs specific behavior.**

WOOP's mechanism is built on a *specific, feasible wish* and a *specific, identifiable obstacle* met with a *specific if-then plan*. Its wins are on concrete near-term behaviors (grades this term, attendance, curbing procrastination, alcohol reduction). It is not evidence that you can "achieve financial literacy" or "achieve leadership" as a year-long unit.

This connects to goal-setting theory's own findings:

- Locke & Latham: specific + difficult goals beat vague goals - **but for highly complex tasks, single outcome goals are ineffective**; complex tasks need multiple goals and respect for the task-complexity moderator. ([ScienceDirect overview](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/social-sciences/goal-setting-theory))
- Seijts & Latham: for tasks requiring *knowledge/skill acquisition* (which a broad capacity is), a **specific challenging LEARNING goal** (focus on discovering strategies) should be set, and a premature **performance-outcome goal can inhibit learning**. ([Seijts & Latham AME 2005 PDF](https://www-2.rotman.utoronto.ca/facbios/file/22%20-%20Seijts%20&%20Latham%20AME%202005.pdf); ["Knowing when to set learning vs performance goals"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0090261611000751))

### Verdict for Q1

**WOOP: well-supported** as a technique, including for children, with the honest caveat that the effect is small-to-medium and likely smaller in app/document delivery.

**Applying WOOP to a broad year-long capacity: sound ONLY if WOOP is used to scaffold a concrete next behavior inside the capacity**, not to "complete the capacity." The right Compass move is: capacity -> a concrete, learner-chosen wish this term -> WOOP that. The "what success looks like" line is defensible for a *concrete behavior* but should not be attached to the capacity as a whole.

**Confidence: well-supported** (WOOP mechanism + child RCT); **mixed** on the "broad capacity as a single goal" framing, where theory actively warns against outcome goals on complex tasks.

## Question 2 - Creator Mindset Goals: Are dispositions goal-able, or is goal-shaping them a category error?

### Findings, disposition by disposition

**Growth mindset - CONTESTED, leaning weak.**
- Sisk et al. (2018), two meta-analyses: overall intervention effect on achievement d = 0.08 (small); benefit concentrated in low-SES and at-risk students. ([Sisk et al. 2018, Psychological Science](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0956797617739704))
- Yeager et al. (2019), National Study of Learning Mindsets, ~12,500 9th-graders, Nature: real but modest effect, improving grades for lower-achieving students and advanced-course-taking, **conditional on supportive peer/teacher norms**. ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-019-1466-y); [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31391586/))
- Macnamara & Burgoyne (2023), Psychological Bulletin, preregistered, 122 studies, 11 moderators: **effect indistinguishable from zero among studies that best follow best practices (p = 0.666)**; found publication bias, financial-incentive bias, and internal-validity threats; higher-quality studies less likely to show benefit. ([Purdue PDF](https://hhs.purdue.edu/skill-learning-and-performance-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/43/2024/08/2023-90931-004.pdf); [Macnamara & Burgoyne 2022 preprint](https://englelab.gatech.edu/articles/2022/Macnamara%20and%20Burgoyne%20(2022)%20-%20Do%20Growth%20Mindset%20Interventions%20Impact%20Students%E2%80%99%20Academic%20Achievement.pdf))
- Countered by Tipton, Bryan, Yeager et al. (2023) commentary defending heterogeneity/context. ([WashU profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/why-meta-analyses-of-growth-mindset-and-other-interventions-shoul/))
- **Net:** the debate is live and the average effect is small-at-best. Compass's existing hedge is exactly right. Asking a learner to set growth mindset as a *goal with a success line* is the shakiest possible use - it stakes a learner-facing achievement on the most contested construct in the pillar.

**Emotion regulation - a DEVELOPMENTAL CAPACITY, scaffolded not achieved.**
- Gross & Thompson process model: ER is a set of strategies (situation selection/modification, attention deployment, cognitive change, response modulation) applied moment to moment - not an endpoint. ([PMC6989808](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6989808/))
- Adolescence is a *pivotal developmental period* for ER; it develops through co-regulation and gradual withdrawal of caregiver scaffolding (anticipatory coaching, debriefing), not by hitting a deadline. ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352250X21001949); [family co-regulation review](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10567-022-00378-4))
- **Net:** ER is developmentally scaffolded and practiced. A "goal with success line" mis-models it and risks implying a child has "failed" at regulating emotions - a psyche-protection red flag.

**Curiosity - a cultivable disposition (state + trait), not an achievement.**
- Kashdan distinguishes trait curiosity (stable propensity) from state curiosity (momentary); measured via CEI-II and the Five-Dimensional Curiosity Scale. ([Kashdan & Roberts PDF](https://www.acsu.buffalo.edu/~robertsj/kashdan&roberts.jsc.2004.pdf); [5DC scale](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0092656617301149))
- Curiosity is malleable and can be cultivated through everyday practice and supportive environments; it is on the OECD Learning Compass as a construct to *develop*. ([UCSB](https://news.ucsb.edu/2025/021917/how-become-more-curious-person-according-new-research); [OECD Curiosity](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/topics/policy-issues/future-of-education-and-skills/learning-compass-constructs/Curiosity.pdf))
- **Net:** cultivable, yes; completable-by-deadline, no. Practice framing fits; goal-with-success-line does not.

**Responsibility - a disposition developed via transfer of responsibility.**
- No single clean construct measure, but the developmental literature treats responsibility as something *transferred* to the learner through gradual release (see Q3), i.e. a moving target of increasing ownership, not a checkbox. **Confidence: thin** on responsibility specifically as a measured construct.

### The category-error argument (this is the crux)

The cleanest argument does not even depend on the contested mindset literature. It comes from goal-setting theory itself:

> A performance/outcome goal set on a complex, skill-acquisition target can *inhibit* learning and *degrade* performance; the correct instrument is a learning goal focused on strategies/process. (Seijts & Latham)

Dispositions are the extreme case of "complex skill acquisition over developmental time." They have:
- no natural finish line (a disposition is never "done"),
- state-by-state expression that fluctuates (curiosity, emotion regulation),
- development driven by scaffolding and practice, not deadline pressure,
- and, for growth mindset specifically, a contested and small average effect that makes any learner-facing "success" claim unsupportable.

Attaching a "success line" to a disposition therefore commits two errors at once: (1) it imposes a performance-outcome goal where a learning goal is indicated (theory says this backfires), and (2) it manufactures a completion event for something that has no completion - which risks a learner concluding they "failed" at being curious or regulated. That is both an evidence error and a psyche-protection concern.

### Verdict for Q2

**YES - treating a disposition as a "goal with a success line" is a category error.**

**Recommended alternative:** reframe the Creator Mindset object from *goal + success line* to a **practice / learning-goal object**:
- a chosen *way of engaging* (a learning goal: "notice and name one emotion when it rises," "ask one question I don't know the answer to"),
- recurring, low-stakes, repeatable - not a deadline,
- movement shown as *narrative pattern over time* (Q3), never a completion state.

Capacities (Life Skills) can plausibly take a year-goal *when decomposed to concrete behaviors*; **dispositions should be scaffolded and practiced, not goal-set to a deadline.**

**Confidence:** the category-error verdict is **well-supported** via goal-setting theory's own learning-vs-performance distinction and developmental ER/curiosity literature. The growth-mindset piece is **contested** and argues *in the same direction*.

## Question 3 - Graduation Not Engagement: showing movement without score/streak/leaderboard

### Findings

**The validated frame is scaffold fading / gradual release of responsibility (GRR).**
- Rooted in Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development. Van de Pol et al. (2010) name three hallmarks of effective scaffolding: **contingency** (support tailored to current need), **fading** (gradual withdrawal), and **transfer of responsibility** (control shifts to the learner). ([Structural Learning](https://www.structural-learning.com/post/vygotskys-theory); [Simply Psychology ZPD](https://www.simplypsychology.org/zone-of-proximal-development.html))
- GRR sequence: "I do" -> "We do" -> "You do together" -> "You do alone." Movement = progressing along this sequence, i.e. **reduced reliance on scaffolds**. ([SaskOER scaffolding ch.](https://www.saskoer.ca/teachingdiverselearners/chapter/scaffolding-2/))
- So "measuring reduced reliance / scaffold fading" **is** a recognized, validated *pedagogical* approach to progress on things that don't complete. **Confidence: well-supported as pedagogy.**

**The operationalization hazard is documented.**
- Fading is "particularly difficult to implement... if not carefully managed, can leave students dependent rather than autonomous." ([ResearchGate scaffolded assessment](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/394748262)) Poorly managed measurement of fading either removes support too early or turns "reduced reliance" into a de-facto score.
- Gamification evidence: the classic Mekler et al. finding is that points/levels/leaderboards raised *performance* but did **not** improve intrinsic motivation, autonomy, or competence. ([U Copenhagen](https://researchprofiles.ku.dk/en/publications/do-points-levels-and-leaderboards-harm-intrinsic-motivation-an-em); [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2583008.2583017)) The real risk is the **overjustification effect**: adding external reward markers to something a learner already finds interesting can *reduce* intrinsic motivation and drop engagement below baseline once removed. Shallow "BPL" (badges/points/leaderboards) is a primary reason gamification underperforms.

### How to operationalize without becoming the metric it avoids

1. **Show the learner their own history, not a number.** Movement = a visible pattern of their own practice over time ("you have named an emotion in the moment 6 times this month, up from 1"). This is a *count of practice acts the learner chose to log*, framed as a story, not a score to maximize.
2. **Make the axis "reliance," not "achievement."** Surface which scaffolds the learner still uses vs no longer needs - graduation is *needing less help*, and it is described, not scored.
3. **No comparison, ever.** Private to the learner; no cross-learner ranking; no streak that punishes a missed day (streaks reintroduce the overjustification/loss-aversion trap).
4. **Let the learner name the movement.** Reflection prompts ("what got easier?") keep the learner as the interpreter of their own progress, which also keeps it autonomy-supportive (Q4).
5. **The measure must be invisible-by-default and never the reward.** The moment a fading indicator becomes the thing the learner optimizes, it has become the metric. Keep it descriptive, retrospective, and unranked.

### Verdict for Q3

**Scaffold fading / reduced reliance is a validated approach** to progress-without-completion, and it is the right frame for dispositions. **Confidence: well-supported as pedagogy; thin on "how to quantify reduced reliance in an app without it becoming a score"** - this is a design-craft problem, not a solved measurement problem. Operationalize as private narrative of the learner's own practice, framed as needing-less-help, never ranked.

## Question 4 - Autonomy Support (SDT): keeping goal-setting learner-owned

### Findings

- SDT (Deci & Ryan) rests on three basic needs: **autonomy, competence, relatedness**. Autonomous motivation (self-endorsed) predicts better learning and wellbeing; **controlling contexts undermine wellbeing** in and out of the classroom. ([Ryan & Deci 2020](https://stial.ie/resources/Ryan%20and%20Deci%202020%20self%20determination%20theory.pdf); [Guay 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/08295735211055355); [Niemiec & Ryan 2009](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2009_NiemiecRyan_TRE.pdf))
- Autonomy-supportive practices in the literature: meaningful choice, providing a rationale for tasks, acknowledging feelings/perspective, using non-controlling language, minimizing external rewards and pressure, and being responsive to the learner's own goals. Even **tone of voice** matters - controlling prosody produces worse reactions in children. ([motivational prosody RCT](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10952248/))
- **Confidence: well-supported.** SDT is one of the most robust frameworks in educational motivation.

### Design features that preserve sovereignty in Compass

1. **Learner picks the goal and the words.** The disposition/capacity may be offered by the pillar, but the *specific practice or wish* and its phrasing come from the learner (self-endorsement).
2. **Offer, never assign.** Present dispositions as invitations with a rationale ("here is why people practice this"), and let the learner opt in, defer, or decline.
3. **No rewards, no pressure markers.** No points/streaks/leaderboards (also supported by the overjustification evidence in Q3). Feedback is informational, not evaluative.
4. **Non-controlling language and tone** throughout, including in-app copy and any voice.
5. **The learner interprets their own progress.** Reflection over verdict; the app surfaces the pattern, the learner names its meaning.
6. **Real exit.** The learner can drop or change a goal without penalty - a controlled system removes this; a sovereign one preserves it.

### Verdict for Q4

**Well-supported.** SDT gives a clean, evidence-backed checklist and it is fully compatible with the recommended Life Skills / Creator Mindset split. Autonomy support is the sovereignty spine of the whole model.

## VERDICT on the Category-Error Crux

**Treating a disposition (growth mindset, emotional regulation, curiosity, responsibility) as a "goal with a success line" IS a category error.**

Two independent lines converge:
1. **Goal-setting theory's own logic** (Seijts & Latham; Locke & Latham task-complexity moderator): outcome/performance goals impair complex skill acquisition; the indicated instrument is a *learning goal* (process/strategy focus) or scaffolded practice. Dispositions are the extreme complex-skill case with no finish line.
2. **Developmental + construct evidence**: emotion regulation and curiosity are cultivated state-by-state over developmental time via scaffolding and practice; growth mindset's average effect is small-at-best and contested (Macnamara & Burgoyne 2023), making a learner-facing "success" claim on it unsupportable.

Adding a success line to a disposition both (a) misapplies an outcome goal where a learning goal belongs, and (b) fabricates a completion event for something uncompletable, risking a "failed to be curious/regulated" experience - an evidence error and a psyche-protection concern at once.

**Recommended alternative for Creator Mindset:** replace *goal + success line* with a **practice object** - a learner-chosen, recurring, low-stakes way of engaging (a learning goal), with movement shown as private narrative of reduced reliance / accumulating practice, never a completion state or score.

**Life Skills capacities may keep a year-goal** - but decompose the capacity to concrete, learner-chosen behaviors and apply WOOP to those; do not attach a success line to the whole capacity.

---

## Design Implications for Compass

1. **Split the object model by pillar.**
   - LIFE SKILLS (capacities) -> keep the goal object, but the "goal" and its success line attach to a **concrete behavior chosen this term**, not the whole capacity. WOOP fits here.
   - CREATOR MINDSET (dispositions) -> replace the goal-with-success-line with a **practice / learning-goal object** (way of engaging + noticing movement), no deadline, no completion state.

2. **Keep - and extend - the growth-mindset hedge.** The existing "not magic on its own" copy is correct per the 2018-2023 meta-analytic debate. Given Macnamara & Burgoyne (2023), do *not* make growth mindset a learner-facing goal to "achieve"; make it a practice/framing at most. This is the single most contested construct in the model.

3. **Show movement as private, narrative reduced-reliance - never a score, streak, or leaderboard.** Scaffold fading is validated pedagogy; the overjustification and gamification evidence warns that any external reward marker can backfire. Surface the learner's own practice history and needing-less-help, framed as story, unranked, with the learner interpreting it.

4. **Bake SDT autonomy-support into both pillars:** learner picks and phrases the goal/practice; offer-not-assign with rationale; no rewards/pressure; informational not evaluative feedback; frictionless exit.

5. **WOOP delivery caveat:** in-app (document-style) WOOP sits at the *lower* end of the effect range (g ~ 0.28 vs 0.47 in-person). Where possible add a light human/relational touch (guide, co-regulation) to recover some of the in-person advantage - which also serves SDT relatedness.
